VCS-1 · The register

Twenty builds we have found most useful.

This is not a catalogue and it is not a price list you order from. It is the work that has come back most often, sized and priced so you can see the shape of a decision before you book a call.

We are a custom shop. What we build for your firm is what your firm actually needs, and the recommendation comes after we have seen your close, not before. Read these as worked examples of what that has looked like twenty times.

How to read this.

Close and reconcile

The heaviest work in the firm and the lane with the most proof behind it. Everything here carries the acceptance test, because everything here touches a general ledger.

01Reconciliation and Cleanup EngineThe firm’s categorization logic, run as a system, with an audit trail on every reclass.Heavy$12,000.00

Ingests transaction detail, applies the firm’s own categorization logic, and returns three things: the cleaned ledger, the exception list, and an audit trail of every reclass with the reason it was made. The associate stops rebuilding the workbook and starts reviewing exceptions.

ReplacesThe first two weeks of the month.
What it needs from youTransaction detail, the current chart of accounts, the categorization rules as they actually run, and one closed month already known good.
Build time from complete intake4 to 8 weeks
Hours returned per month24 to 40
Value returned per year$21,600.00 to $48,000.00
Pays for itself in3 to 7 months
Deep dive
How it runs
  1. 01Reads the period’s transaction detail from the bank, card and ledger exports as the firm already pulls them, with no change to how the data leaves the source system.
  2. 02Applies the firm’s categorization rules in the firm’s own order of precedence, including the client-specific overrides that currently live in one person’s memory.
  3. 03Splits the period into three buckets: categorized with confidence, categorized and flagged for review, and not categorized at all. Nothing is silently guessed into the second bucket.
  4. 04Writes the reclass log, one line per decision, naming the rule that fired and the transaction it fired on.
  5. 05Rolls the prior period forward so the same rules and the same overrides run next month without being rebuilt.
What it hands back
  • The cleaned ledger, in the firm’s import format.
  • The exception list, ranked, with a stated reason per line.
  • The reclass audit trail.
  • A rollforward file that carries the rules into the next period.
How it is accepted

Reproduces a month the firm has already closed and signed, category for category, to the penny. The known-good month is chosen by the firm, not by us, and it is run before the engine sees a live period.

What it does not do

It does not invent a chart of accounts, and it does not decide policy. Where the firm’s rules conflict, it reports the conflict rather than picking a side. It does not monitor the bank feed between periods.

What care covers

Format remaps when a bank or a platform changes its export, model compatibility when a version ships, and break-fix. New rules and new entities are quoted as work, flagged in the artifact in one line.

Why it is on the shelf

Named in the practice’s own library review as its strongest single asset, and the one rebuilt the most times.

This is the build with regulatory teeth, which is why the acceptance test matters more here than anywhere else on the menu.

ReplacesThe reconciliation nobody wants to own and every state bar can ask to see.
What it needs from youTrust bank statements, the trust ledger, client ledger detail, and one closed month already known good.

Deep dive
How it runs
  1. 01Reconciles the trust bank balance to the trust ledger balance to the sum of client ledger balances, which is the three-way test the rules actually require.
  2. 02Tests every client sub-ledger for a negative balance, which is the finding that ends careers, and reports it first.
  3. 03Ages outstanding items and flags stale checks and unidentified deposits against the firm’s own threshold.
  4. 04Produces the reconciliation in a form that can be handed to a bar auditor without being retyped.

  • The outstanding items schedule with ageing.
  • The exception list with the resolution recorded against each item.

Reproduces a period the firm has already reconciled and signed, all three legs agreeing, before it touches a live one. Where the historical reconciliation was wrong, the acceptance run says so, and that finding belongs to the firm.

What it does not do

It does not move money, it does not clear checks, and it is not a legal opinion on the jurisdiction’s trust rules. It applies the firm’s stated policy and reports where the policy is silent.

What care covers

Priority care is the correct tier for this build, because the monthly Tie-Out is the artifact the firm wants on file when the question is asked.

Why it is on the shelf

Anchors the legal lane alongside the Clio to QBO bridge, and is usually sold with it.

  1. 01Walks the firm’s own close checklist in the firm’s own order, not a generic one.
  2. 02Tests each account against its expected state: reconciled, supported, within tolerance of prior period, or explained.
  3. 03Separates three findings the reviewer currently has to separate by hand: this tied, this did not tie, and this needs judgment.

It does not sign the close and it does not replace the reviewer. It produces the first pass so the reviewer starts at judgment instead of at tick-and-tie.

What care covers

Checklist changes are the firm’s to make; the build reads the checklist as data, so a changed line does not require a rebuild.

Why it is on the shelf

Three paid clients already behind this pattern, which makes it the easiest yes on the menu.
