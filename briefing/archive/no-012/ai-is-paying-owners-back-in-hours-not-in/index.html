AI is paying owners back in hours, not in sales.

What moved

The Philadelphia Fed published new Small Business Credit Survey results on September 22. Nationally, 46% of small employer firms used AI in fall 2025. In the Fed's own region, nearly 80% of adopters point it at writing or marketing. Yet over 70% saw no change in sales or labor costs, while 71% reported higher productivity. The same day, Homebase reported its own survey: owners using AI for scheduling and payroll save 7.7 hours a month.

What it changes

The return on AI at your size is time, and it lands in the back office first. Writing ads with ChatGPT feels productive, but the Fed data says sales mostly held flat. Scheduling, payroll and timesheets are repetitive, rule-bound and easy to check. That is where hours come back. Homebase sells scheduling and payroll software, so treat its 7.7 hours as a vendor number. The Fed's productivity figure points the same way.

What not to do

Do not judge your AI spend by how many posts it writes; count the hours it returns. Do not expect AI to lift sales on its own; the Fed found most adopters saw no change. Do not buy a new platform first; test ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini on one scheduling or payroll task. Do not let the saved hours vanish into email; put them on the calendar for selling.

Point it at the paperwork

Most owners start with AI where it is most fun: ads, posts, newsletters. The Fed's numbers show where that leads. Lots of drafts, flat sales. Marketing output is easy to make and hard to tie to revenue. Back-office work is different. A schedule either covers the shifts or it does not. A timesheet either matches the punch log or it does not. That makes the work easy to hand off and easy to check. It is why productivity rose while sales stayed put. AI shortens the work you already do. It does not create demand by itself.

So run it backwards. List the admin jobs that eat your week, then measure each in minutes. Start with the schedule, the timesheet check and the payroll questions staff send you. Give each one a written prompt in ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, and a person who checks the result. If you already pay for scheduling or payroll software, turn on its AI features before you buy anything new. The trade-off is the first two weeks, spent writing prompts and checking output. After that the hours come back every week. Spend them on customers, because the AI will not.

The play

  1. You, this week: log every scheduling, timesheet and payroll task for five days, with the minutes each one takes. One notes app is enough.
  2. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini with next week's availability: "Build a shift schedule from these staff availabilities and these coverage needs. Flag any gap or anyone over 40 hours. Do not invent availability."
  3. Your office lead: ask your payroll and scheduling vendors which AI features you already pay for, and switch on one this month.
  4. Paste the timesheet export with this prompt: "Compare these timesheets with the schedule. List every mismatch over 15 minutes, by name and date. Do not change any numbers."
  5. You: block the hours you get back on your calendar for sales calls and customer visits. Treat them as meetings.
  6. Run The Hours Back on this page to price what your admin time costs you a year.
