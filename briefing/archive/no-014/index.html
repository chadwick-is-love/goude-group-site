01 / Works nowBrief · 45 sec

Most owners now run AI. Two in five pay nothing for it.

What moved

On October 2 the U.S. Chamber of Commerce published its fifth Impact of Technology on U.S. Small Business report. Teneo surveyed 3,732 firms under 250 employees in July and August. 66% use AI, up from 58% in 2025 and 23% in 2023. On October 8 the New York Fed reported on 5,248 employer firms surveyed last fall: 46% use AI, 31% of users report higher sales, and 77% saw no change in labor costs.

What it changes

Running without AI now puts you in the minority, and the jobs that pay are ordinary. The Chamber's users put it on marketing (41%), customer experience (35%) and accounting (31%). By the Chamber's count, 41% of users pay nothing and the median paying firm spends $250 a month. That is a quarter-long test with no budget line. The 31% who report higher sales is a self-report, so your own score matters more than theirs.

What not to do

Do not buy a platform before the free tier has run one job for 30 days. Do not read the Chamber's 7-point growth gap as cause; firms already growing were likelier to adopt, and the report measures correlation. Do not take Homebase's $343 a month as your number; it is the vendor's survey of its own market and an owner's estimate. Do not hand AI the books or payroll without a person checking every entry for the first month.

The tape

Sep 22Homebase publishes its 2026 Main Street AI Gap Report, 750 U.S. decision makers at hourly-team businesses. 74% use or pilot AI, up from 64%. Owners using it for scheduling and payroll estimate 7.7 hours and $343 saved a month. The vendor's figures.
Oct 2The U.S. Chamber releases its fifth Impact of Technology on U.S. Small Business report, 3,732 firms surveyed by Teneo. 66% use AI. Users report sales growth 7 points more often than non-users, 89% to 82%. 47% say AI is creating jobs at their firm; 6% say it cut headcount.
Oct 8The New York Fed analyzes the 2025 Small Business Credit Survey, 5,248 employer firms. 46% use AI. 31% of users report increased sales and 77% report no change in labor costs. AI users expect hiring and revenue growth more often, 10 to 14 points after controls.
NextThe Fed's 2026 survey is in the field this fall and reports next spring. Until then the 31% is the number to beat. Pick one job, score it for 30 days, and decide on your own figure, not a survey's.

The first job is the cheap one

Adoption jumped because the entry got cheap, not because owners got technical. The Chamber's users mostly run tools someone else built: 64% use externally developed AI, and the most common type is a generative chatbot, used by 41%. The first jobs, a customer email and a marketing post, need no integration and no IT. That is why two in five users pay nothing. The catch sits in the Fed's data: only 51% of users have built AI into a process. Using ChatGPT for one email is not a process. The sales show up where the job repeats every week with a person owning it.

So pick one repeating job and run it for 30 days on the tool you already have: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot. Set the score before day one. For marketing, leads a week. For replies, office hours a week. For the books, days to close the month. The trade-off is data: the free tier runs on consumer terms, so customer lists, card numbers and pay rates stay out of it, and anything touching the books goes on a paid business seat. For scheduling and payroll, check the tool you pay for first. QuickBooks, Gusto and Homebase shipped AI inside the product, and most owners never switched it on.

The play

  1. Monday, you: pick one job from the three the majority already use, marketing copy, customer replies or the books. One job, one owner, 30 days, nothing else changes.
  2. Before you buy anything, open the settings of what you already pay for. QuickBooks, Gusto and Homebase all shipped AI features inside the product. Switch them on first.
  3. Paste into ChatGPT or Claude: "Here are my last ten customer emails and my replies. Learn my tone. Draft replies to the next five in that tone, under 120 words, and flag any that mention price or a complaint for a person."
  4. Write the score before day one. Marketing: leads a week. Replies: office hours a week, timed. Books: days from month end to close. Put the before number on paper.
  5. Keep customer lists, card numbers and pay rates out of free consumer accounts. If the job touches the books or customer data, pay for a business seat so your data is not training data.
  6. Use The Thirty-Day Start on this page to write the plan, the first prompt and the house rules. On day 30, keep it only if the score moved.

02 / BewareBrief · 45 sec

AI wrote the email script. 95,000 customers saw each other's addresses.

What moved

On September 30 Singapore's privacy regulator confirmed its first AI-related data breach. A marketing employee at Bee Cheng Hiang, a food retailer, asked a generative AI tool to write a Python script for a bulk email. On April 25 it sent 95,364 customer addresses in batches of 1,000, every address visible in the To field. The regulator says the AI did not malfunction. The prompt never said hide recipients, and nobody opened a test email.

What it changes

The regulator judged the process, not the model. No test send, no second reviewer, no policy for staff using AI. On September 2 the company signed an undertaking: independent technical review of any AI-written code that touches personal data, test sends to dummy accounts, an automated block on multiple addresses in one recipient field. The fine cap there is S$1 million or 10% of local turnover. Your customers read a wrong send the same way, in any country.

What not to do

Do not let a ten-minute AI script replace the mail tool you already pay for, which hides recipients by default. Do not accept a log as proof; the employee here checked the activity log and never read the email. Do not let the person who built an AI script be the only person who tests it. Do not treat this as a hallucination story. The AI did exactly what it was asked, and the ask was one sentence short.

The tape

Apr 25A Bee Cheng Hiang marketing employee runs an AI-written Python script to email the member list. It sends batches of 1,000 with every address in the To field, exposing 95,364 members. The company reports it to the PDPC two days later.
Sep 2The PDPC accepts a voluntary undertaking. The company commits to independent technical review of AI-generated code handling personal data, test sends to dummy accounts, training, a written breach procedure, and an automated block on multiple addresses in one recipient field.
Sep 30The PDPC tells The Straits Times this is the first AI-related breach reported in Singapore. Its findings: the AI did not malfunction, the prompt omitted hiding recipients, testing relied on logs, no supervisor reviewed the work, and no AI use policy existed.
Oct 1Bloomberg, Mothership and VnExpress carry the story. The company now requires at least two staff to verify every bulk email before it goes out. No fine is reported. The law allows up to S$1 million or 10% of Singapore turnover.
NextThe PDPC says it will verify compliance and can issue a direction if the company falls short. No equivalent US ruling exists yet, so the standard your customers and your state regulator will apply is the one written here: test the output, and have a second person read it.

The tool did what it was told

Owners worry about AI making things up. This breach was the other failure, the boring one. The script was written in minutes, the logic was almost right, and a misplaced bracket grouped each batch of 1,000 addresses into one To field. The employee tested it by reading the activity log, which said every email went out. It did. Nobody opened one. A script also skips every guardrail your mail platform already has, like blind copy by default and recipient caps. This was the company's first use of AI in operations, so there was no rule saying who reads the output before it ships.

The fix is cheap and slightly slow. Bulk sends stay inside Mailchimp, Constant Contact, HubSpot or your CRM. Anything a staffer builds with AI that touches customer data gets two signatures, and the second one comes from someone who reads the output, not the log. Before a real send, the script goes to three dummy addresses you own, and someone opens them and looks at the To line. The trade-off is a day of delay on a campaign and some lost enthusiasm from the person who built it in ten minutes. Against a notice to 95,364 customers and a year of regulator check-ins, that is a cheap day.

The play

  1. You, today: list every script, macro, Zapier, Make or n8n flow that sends to customers or touches customer data, and note which ones a staffer built with AI. That list is the scope of the rule.
  2. Your office manager, this week: move every bulk send back into the mail tool you already pay for, Mailchimp, Constant Contact, HubSpot or your CRM. It hides recipients by default. A script is the exception that needs a signature.
  3. Set the two-person rule: nothing AI-built that touches customer data goes live until a second person reads the output. Reading the log does not count. Opening the test email does.
  4. Before any real send, run the script against three dummy addresses you own. Open each one. Look at the To line. Takes five minutes, and it is the exact test that was skipped.
  5. Paste into ChatGPT or Claude with the script: "My team asked an AI to write this script to email our customer list. Tell me in plain words what each recipient will see in the To, CC and BCC fields, what customer data leaves our systems, and what goes wrong if the list has a bad entry."
  6. Run The Second Pair on this page to write the one-page rule, name the reviewer, and post it where the team builds things.

03 / PriceBrief · 45 sec

Claude Max and Team plans now include API credit. Claim it now.

What moved

On October 7 Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 at 10 cents per million input tokens and 50 cents per million output, for prompts up to 100,000 tokens. That is 90 percent below Haiku 4.5. Sonnet 5.5 cache reads fell by half, to 10 cents. The same day Anthropic began adding monthly API credits to two plans: $100 on Max 5x, $200 on Max 20x, and $20 or $100 per Team seat, pooled and capped at $500.

What it changes

If your team already pays for Claude Max or Team, the seat now carries money you can spend on automations in Zapier, Make or n8n. On Max the credit equals the seat price. It does not pay for Claude Code or the chat app, and it expires at the end of every billing cycle. The model price cut means a tagging or sorting step costs a fraction of a cent, so the credit covers more runs than most teams this size send in a month.

What not to do

Do not leave the credit unclaimed; nobody links the account for you, and the first month's balance expires unused. Do not link your plan to a developer's Console organization you do not control; the link is one to one and only Anthropic support can undo it. Do not expect it to cover Claude Code or extra usage in the app; it spends on the API only. Do not put Haiku 5.5 on writing a customer reads without checking it against Sonnet 5.5 first. Do not hand out one API key with no workspace spend limit.

The tape

Jun 15Zapier moves AI by Zapier steps to model-tier pricing: Standard models cost 1 task a run, Advanced 3, Premium 5. Claude Haiku 4.5 sits in Advanced. Bringing your own API key costs 1 task on any tier.
Oct 6An ActuIA survey of list prices puts Claude Haiku 4.5 at $1 input and $5 output per million tokens, against OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna at 10 cents and 50 cents. Anthropic's small model costs ten times OpenAI's.
Oct 7Anthropic releases Claude Haiku 5.5 at 10 cents input and 50 cents output per million tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens, matching Luna. Sonnet 5.5 cache reads drop from 20 cents to 10 cents.
Oct 8The monthly API credit rolls out over several days: $100 on Max 5x, $200 on Max 20x, $20 per Team Standard seat, $100 per Premium seat, pooled and capped at $500. New subscribers wait seven days. Unused credit expires each cycle.
NextZapier's tier page, updated October 1, still lists Haiku 4.5 at 3 tasks a run and does not list Haiku 5.5. Anthropic has given the credit program no end date. Check both before you move a workflow.

The seat price now buys two things

Your Claude subscription has always covered two separate meters. The seat pays for chat, Projects and Claude Code. The API, where a Zap or a Make scenario sends its request, bills per token on a separate account with its own card. Until October 7 a shop paying $25 a seat for chat also paid per run for every automation. Now the seat funds both, up to the credit. The Zapier part matters more than it looks. AI by Zapier charges 3 tasks a run on Advanced models and 5 on Premium. Bring your own key and every run drops to 1 task, with the model cost landing on the credit.

Claim it this week, then wire it. The Max subscriber, or a Team owner, links a Console organization under Settings and Billing. The balance appears under Promotional credits. Create one API key per automation tool, never one key for everyone. Give each project its own workspace with a spend limit, because every key draws from the same pool. Put Haiku 5.5 on sorting, tagging and summarising. Keep Sonnet 5.5 on anything a customer reads. The trade-off is that a key is a password. Rotate it when someone leaves. When the balance hits zero, requests stop rather than billing your plan. The program has no stated end date, so budget it month to month.

The play

  1. You, today: on claude.ai open Settings, then Billing (Max) or Organization settings, then Billing (Team owner). Under API credits, link a Console organization you own and accept the terms. The balance shows under Promotional credits at platform.claude.com.
  2. Whoever runs your Zaps: create an API key in that Console organization and add it to each AI by Zapier step as bring your own key. Each run drops to 1 task and the model cost bills to the credit.
  3. Same person: in the Console, give each automation its own workspace with a monthly spend limit, so one runaway Zap cannot drain the pool before payroll runs.
  4. Paste into ChatGPT or Claude: "Here is a list of our Zapier and Make automations that use an AI step: [paste]. For each one, say whether a small fast model is enough or whether the output goes to a customer and needs the stronger model. Then order them by how many runs a month."
  5. Your office manager: set a calendar reminder for the day after each billing date to check the balance landed, since credit does not roll over and nothing warns you when it expires.
  6. Run The Credit Clock on this page to total what your seats hand back each month and how many automation runs that buys at the new prices.

04 / Skip itBrief · 45 sec

Skip the chatbot with no human exit. Customers give it ten minutes.

What moved

On October 14 Five9 published its 2026 Consumer CX Report, a survey of 3,000 consumers in the US, UK and Germany run in April by Hanover Research. Five9 sells AI contact center software, so the numbers are a vendor's. 92 percent give an AI agent under ten minutes before asking for a person. Trust that the AI will solve the problem is 55 percent with a visible human option and 26 percent without.

What it changes

The chat widget you were pitched is sold on deflection, meaning fewer calls and fewer emails. The survey says the customer who cannot find a person stops trusting the answer, and 40 percent say AI service makes them less likely to use a company at all. The meter runs either way. Intercom's Fin bills $0.99 when the customer confirms the answer or leaves without asking for more. A customer who gave up counts as resolved.

What not to do

Do not install a chat box with no phone number and no talk to a person button on its first screen. Do not let the bot handle refunds, disputes or complaints alone; route those to a named person. Do not hide that it is a bot; 71 percent say being told matters a lot. Do not sign a per-resolution contract before asking in writing what counts as resolved when the customer walks away. Do not judge the widget by chats deflected; count bookings and solved tickets.

The tape

Oct 2025Qualtrics surveys more than 20,000 consumers in 14 countries for its 2026 Consumer Experience Trends report. Nearly one in five who used AI for customer service got no benefit from it, almost four times the rate for AI use in general.
Jun 24Five9 releases the first findings from its April survey of 3,000 consumers and 600 service leaders, run by Hanover Research. Two-thirds prefer speaking with a person, and 71 percent say it matters a lot to be told when they are talking to AI.
Oct 14Five9 publishes the full consumer report. 92 percent give an AI agent under ten minutes. Trust in the answer runs 55 percent with a visible human option, 26 percent without. 40 percent say AI service makes them less likely to use a company at all.
Oct 18Price check this week: Intercom's Fin runs from $0.99 per outcome with no seats, and its help pages count a resolution when the customer confirms the answer or leaves without asking for more. Tidio's Lyro starts at $32.50 a month for 50 AI conversations.
NextOpen your own widget as a customer tonight. If you cannot find a phone number or a person within two messages, fix that before you renew. Then ask the vendor in writing what it counts as resolved when the customer walks away.

The meter runs when the customer quits

Here is the part the demo skips. A chat widget's success metric is deflection, the share of chats a person never touched. Deflection counts the customer who got an answer and the customer who gave up the same way. Intercom's help pages spell it out: a Fin resolution is counted when the customer confirms the answer or exits without asking for more, and each one bills $0.99. Return to that chat later and the charge is removed. Call a competitor instead and it stands. Five9 found 46 percent of people who switched to a human did it because the bot kept misunderstanding them. Your report calls those chats contained.

Keep the bot and change the exit. Put your phone number and a talk to a person button on the first screen of the widget, and set the handoff to fire after two misunderstandings, not ten minutes. Five9's number says trust more than doubles when the person is visible, so the bot ends up answering more, not less. Let it handle hours, directions, order status and price ranges. Send refunds, disputes and complaints to a named person inside business hours. The trade-off is real: a visible exit means more chats reach your desk in the first month. Count them. Most are the chats you were losing anyway.

The play

  1. You, tonight: open your website on your phone, start a chat, and count the taps to a phone number or a person. More than two is a fail.
  2. Your web person: put the phone number and a Talk to a person button on the widget's first screen, and set the handoff to trigger after two misunderstood or unanswered turns.
  3. Paste your current bot instructions into ChatGPT or Claude with: "Rewrite these chatbot instructions for a [type of business]. It must say it is an AI in its first line, answer only hours, directions, pricing ranges and order status, and offer a person by name and phone number whenever it cannot answer in two tries or the customer mentions a refund, a dispute or a complaint."
  4. Your office manager: pull last month's chat transcripts and sort them into three piles: answered, handed to a person, and customer left. The third pile is your real deflection rate.
  5. Email the vendor before renewal and ask what counts as a resolution when the customer stops replying, and whether an abandoned chat is billed. Get the answer in writing.
  6. Use The Exit Rule on this page to write the widget rules, the first line the bot says, and the vendor questions in one document.

05 / Steal thisBrief · 45 sec

Your HubSpot CRM now updates itself from calls and emails.

What moved

On September 16, at its UNBOUND conference in Boston, HubSpot shipped a self-updating Smart CRM that captures calls, emails and meetings on its own, Context Home, a page holding what HubSpot knows about your business, and a rebuilt Breeze Assistant. The Notetaker behind the capture joins Google Meet, Teams and Zoom on Sales Hub Professional: $90 a seat a month billed annually, $100 monthly, $1,500 onboarding. Context Home and inbox logging work on every plan, free included.

What it changes

The Monday pipeline review stops being a data entry session. Every booked meeting gets a summary, a transcript and a list of suggested record changes, each tied to the moment in the call that produced it. A person approves each one with a click. Starter gets less: the inbox logs threads and a beta writes contact details from email signatures into records with no review step. The exposure is reach: anyone with access to a record sees what the AI wrote.

What not to do

Do not tick the Automated box on deal properties in week one; Call outcome and Meeting outcome are automated by default, and that is enough until the suggestions prove out. Do not connect an owner inbox that holds payroll, bank or lawyer threads before the account-wide Never Log list is set; tracked mail is visible to other users. Do not turn on New Contact Extraction until someone has read a week of enrichment changes. Do not record a patient call; Notetaker does not cover protected health information.

The tape

Sep 16HubSpot opens UNBOUND in Boston and ships the Fall 2026 Spotlight: a self-updating Smart CRM, Context Home and a rebuilt Breeze Assistant. Its Notetaker help page is updated the same day: Professional or Enterprise, review before a property changes, 40 meetings a day per user.
Sep 21Diginomica's product review places Context Home in Agent Hub under the Context tab, with business profile, brand kit, competitors and customer personas. HubSpot's own figure for Professional and Enterprise customers using context: 3.2 times more deals. That is the vendor's number.
Sep 28HubSpot updates its connected inbox page: all products and plans, Gmail access to read, modify, create and send, and tracked correspondence visible to other users on the account. The Never Log list stays the owner's only fence, set account-wide by a Super Admin.
Oct 5The Manage AI context page is updated: available on all products and plans, free included. Super Admin or Partner Admin permission is needed to edit most of it. Custom context takes free text or file uploads, and every agent reads it before working.
NextConversational enrichment, the beta that writes contact details from logged email, costs no credits while in public beta. Deal Progression is labelled Beta on the Sales Hub pricing page. Expect credit charges when either leaves beta, so write down what each one touches now.

The record changes only when a person clicks

Here is the mechanism. Notetaker sits in the meeting, announces itself by audio, chat and a pop-up, and writes a summary and transcript to the meeting record. Smart data capture then reads that transcript and proposes property changes: amount, close date, next step, whoever you pick. Each proposal lands in a Follow-ups section with a Source link to the sentence that produced it. Nothing moves until someone clicks approve, unless a Super Admin ticks Automated on that property. Standard properties cost nothing. A custom property costs 10 credits per transcript run since April 14, and Professional includes 3,000 credits.

Pick one pipeline and one rep. Turn on Notetaker for that seat only, choose five standard properties for capture, leave Automated off, and run two weeks of real meetings. On Friday the owner opens the week's meeting records and reads every suggestion against its Source link before touching the Monday board. The trade-off is money against coverage. Free and Starter log the inbox and fill contact fields, but no meeting gets captured. Professional captures the meeting and costs $90 a seat a month plus $1,500 to start. Buy it for the people who take the calls, not the whole company.

The play

  1. You, today: find your tier under Settings, Account and Billing. Free or Starter gets inbox logging and Context Home. Meeting capture needs Sales Hub Professional, $90 a seat a month billed annually, bought only for the people who take sales calls.
  2. Your Super Admin, before any inbox connects: Settings, Data Management, Objects, Activities, Email Log and Track tab, Never Log. Add your own domain, your bank, your payroll provider, your lawyer and your accountant. It is not retroactive, so do this first.
  3. Each rep: Settings, General, Email, connect the work inbox with inbox automation on, then connect the calendar. Every external meeting is booked through HubSpot with the customer's contact on the invite, or Notetaker will not join.
  4. Super Admin, Professional only: Settings, Notetaker, turn on Conversation recording, Transcription and analysis, and Meetings, for named users only. Then Settings, Data Management, Data Capture, tick Use smart data capture and pick five standard properties. Leave Automated unticked.
  5. Paste this into Breeze Assistant on Monday morning: "List every open deal with a meeting or call logged in the last 7 days. For each one, show the suggested property updates still waiting for approval and the deal owner. Then list open deals with no logged activity in 14 days."
  6. Use The Capture Rules on this page to write the setup checklist for your tier, the consent line, and the Friday review rule. Pin it to the pipeline board, and keep it until two Fridays in a row need no correction.
