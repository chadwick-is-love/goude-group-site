Google Assistant dies September 4. Find your "Hey Google" habits before then.

What moved

Google announced Wednesday that Gemini replaces Google Assistant starting September 4, rolling out over several weeks across Android phones and tablets, Wear OS watches, headphones, and Android Auto, with Google TV, speakers, and smart displays to follow. Once a device switches there is no way back. The retirement was planned for 2025, slipped a year, and now has a date and a user email.

What it changes

If your business runs on Android, the voice layer underneath your workday changes on someone else's schedule: kitchen timers, hands-free calls and navigation in the truck, the front-counter speaker, routines that announce and remind. Gemini converses better and follows deterministic commands differently, so a routine that fired identically for six years may now interpret instead of obey. You get about a month of notice, and older devices reportedly get a slower Gemini permanently.

What not to do

Do not find out on September 5 from a confused crew; inventory the "Hey Google" habits first, they were never written down. Do not build elaborate new voice automations on any assistant right now, since the layer is mid-churn. And do not assume watches, headphones, and car units transition cleanly; test one of each before the fleet updates.

The workflow nobody deployed

Voice assistants entered businesses without a rollout plan: someone said "Hey Google, set a timer for twelve minutes" in 2019 and a kitchen depended on it by 2020. Because these workflows were never deployed, they are not documented, and because they are not documented, nobody knows what breaks on September 4. The inventory is a five-minute standup question: what do you say "Hey Google" for? Timers, calls, navigation, reminders, music, intercom announcements, door locks, thermostats. Write the list; it is shorter than you fear and more load-bearing than you think.

Then stage the test instead of taking the update blind. The rollout runs for weeks, which is a gift: update one phone, one watch, one speaker early, run the list against Gemini, and rebuild or simplify what breaks before the rest of the fleet turns over. The strategic move is the same one the AWS story taught: fewer dependencies on any vendor's assistant layer, not more, until the churn settles.

The play

  1. Ask the team at standup: what do you say "Hey Google" for? Write every answer down, including the embarrassing ones. That list is your migration scope.
  2. Update one device of each type in the first week and run the whole list on it. Note what breaks, what changed behavior, and what needs re-recording.
  3. Rebuild what broke, and simplify while you are in there: any routine wired to locks, alarms, or opening procedures should not depend on a voice model's interpretation.
  4. Brief the crew before September 4 with the note the tool on the right writes, so the change lands as scheduled maintenance instead of a mystery.
