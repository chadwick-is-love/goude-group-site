Insurers want 14 percent more for small-group health cover next year. Start the renewal now.

What moved

KFF published its read of the 2027 rate filings this week: nearly 300 insurers selling small-group coverage across all fifty states are asking for a median 14 percent increase, with 59 percent of them between 10 and 20 percent and six carriers above 30. The filings cite a 10.8 percent underlying medical cost trend, GLP-1 drug spend, specialty biologics, and behavioral health growing more than 20 percent a year. Regulators approve final rates through the fall; the new prices land January 1.

What it changes

Fourteen percent on a six-figure annual premium bill is a five-figure increase, stacked on last year's climb. The filings also explain the quote before it arrives: the fully insured small-group pool has shrunk from 17 million covered lives in 2013 to 10 million, and every healthy group that exits to level-funded raises the price on whoever stays. That is the decision on your desk this fall, stay in the pool, restructure the plan, or change how you contribute, and groups that start in August get quoted options that groups opening the envelope in November do not.

What not to do

Do not wait for the renewal packet; by the time it arrives, alternatives cannot be quoted and implemented before the deadline. Do not jump to level-funded on price alone, because a bad claims year comes back to you; read the stop-loss terms before you sign, not after. And do not cut the benefit silently to absorb the cost. In the labor market article one describes, the health plan is part of the wage, and staff price it that way even when owners do not.

The tape

Aug 6The Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker publishes the analysis: 295 small-group insurers, median proposed increase 14 percent for 2027, the middle half of requests running 10 to 18 percent. Six insurers ask for more than 30.
DriversThe filings themselves name the causes: underlying medical costs trending at 10.8 percent, GLP-1 utilization, new biologics without generic competition, behavioral health claims growing north of 20 percent annually, and dispute-resolution costs from the No Surprises Act adding almost a point at some carriers.
The poolFully insured small-group enrollment has fallen from 17 million people in 2013 to 10 million. Healthier groups keep leaving for level-funded and self-insured arrangements, which concentrates risk on the groups that remain and feeds the next year's increase. The spiral is structural, not cyclical.
The calendarFilings run May through August, state approvals land October and November, rates go live January 1. Approved rates historically come in near or slightly under the requests. The window where a business can actually shop closes when the approvals publish and brokers flood.

August is when the renewal is a negotiation

The renewal packet is designed to be accepted: it arrives late, quotes one option, and prices delay as the default. The counter is to run the process backward. Know your exposure number now, put your broker on notice this month, and require three quotes by October 1: the current plan renewed, one plan-design variation, and a level-funded option with the stop-loss terms attached in writing. Brokers produce alternatives for the clients who ask in August and renewals for the ones who call in November.

Separate the plan decision from the contribution decision. A defined-dollar contribution, the business pays a fixed amount per employee and employees choose their trade-off across two plan options, converts an open-ended 14 percent liability into a budgeted line you control, without a silent benefit cut. It also puts the real conversation on the table: here is what we pay, here are the choices, both said out loud before January instead of discovered in a paycheck.

The play

  1. Put your current premium and headcount into the instrument on the right; hold your own number before the packet holds it for you.
  2. Email the broker this week: three quotes by October 1, renewal, one design change, one level-funded with stop-loss terms in writing.
  3. Decide the contribution structure separately from the plan pick; defined-dollar keeps the budget fixed while employees keep the choice.
  4. Whatever you choose, present it with the compensation picture, not as a benefits memo. Article one says who is reachable in this market; the plan is part of why they stay.
