01 / The InvoiceBrief · 45 sec

The fake permit invoice has your permit number, your job address and your planner's name on it. All three are public records, and that is exactly the point.

What moved

The FBI issued a nationwide alert on March 9 about criminals impersonating city and county permit officials. The campaign never stopped. Nevada County warned again on July 31, Humboldt County on July 14 for the third time this year, and Rapid City on July 2 after four businesses reported the emails. Contractors, developers and business owners are named as the targets.

What it changes

Documented losses run $3,800 to $13,050, sized to sit inside a plausible permit fee. One applicant paid $3,800 and got a second demand for $2,800. Payment is requested by wire, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal or crypto, all of which are gone the moment they leave. Government impersonation losses hit $797.9 million last year across 32,424 complaints, roughly double the year before.

What not to do

Do not verify by checking whether the details look right, because the details are always right. Do not call the number on the invoice or reply to the email, which is the one instruction the sender wants you to follow. Do not let urgency skip the rule, since the deadline pressure in the email is the product, not a coincidence.

The tape

Mar 9The FBI issues alert I-030926-PSA, warning that criminals nationwide are impersonating city and county officials and invoicing permit applicants for wire, peer-to-peer or crypto payment.
Mar 26Baltimore reporting documents a completed loss: an applicant sent $3,800, then received a follow-up demand for $2,800. A Frederick County applicant lost thousands.
Jul 2Rapid City reports at least four businesses received the emails, naming contractors, developers and business owners as the target set.
Jul 14Humboldt County warns for the third time this year. Researchers publish the money trail the same day: 53 mule accounts across 23 campaigns, with 55 percent concentrated in two banks.
Jul 31Nevada County publishes the spoofed sender address and states plainly that it never asks for payment by wire. Three weeks ago, and still running.

Correct details prove nothing at all

Your permit application, the hearing agenda and the case file are public records. Anyone can read your permit number, your job address, your project description and the name and title of the planner assigned to you. So when an invoice arrives carrying all of it, the instinct that fires is exactly backwards: nobody else would know that, therefore this is real. Everybody knows it. It is posted on the county website. Generative tools handle the letterhead. Accuracy is the cheapest part of this fraud to manufacture, which is why the criminals lead with it.

Two things actually discriminate, and neither requires judgment. The sender's domain: real ones end in .gov or .us, and the fakes are near misses like planning.coNevadaCounty.ca@usa.gov, which Nevada County published in July. And the payment channel: no United States city or county accepts wire transfer, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal or cryptocurrency for permit fees. Palm Beach, Sonoma, Humboldt, Nevada County and Rapid City have all said so in writing. If the channel is one of those, you are done deciding. There is nothing to verify.

The play

  1. Say one sentence to whoever pulls permits and whoever pays invoices: no permit fee is ever paid from an emailed invoice, only through the portal we navigate to ourselves or in person at the counter.
  2. Print the rule card the generator writes and tape it where invoices get paid. This works because it removes the judgment call from a person under time pressure.
  3. If a permit invoice is already in the inbox, check two things and nothing else: does the sender end in .gov or .us, and what payment channel is requested.
  4. If money already went out, call the bank fraud line today and file at ic3.gov. The first 48 hours decide whether it is recoverable.

02 / The RefundBrief · 45 sec

Nobody is mailing you the tariff refund. Almost ten thousand approved refunds are sitting frozen because the business never typed its bank details into a portal it has never logged into.

What moved

The Supreme Court ruled in February that the emergency powers act gives no tariff authority, making roughly $166 billion in duties refundable across 53 million entries and more than 330,000 importers. Customs built a self-service portal and pays refunds with interest. As of a July court filing, about $122 billion had been accepted and 9,837 approved refunds were untransmitted purely because no bank details were on file.

What it changes

Roughly 126,000 claims have been filed against 330,000 eligible importers. This is not a lawsuit and not a consultant engagement. It is a call to your customs broker and a file upload. If you imported $120,000 of goods in 2025, the duty at rates between 10 and 50 percent plausibly ran $15,000 to $45,000. Refunds arrive in 60 to 90 days, with interest.

What not to do

Do not wait for it to arrive on its own, because customs does not push money to anyone who has not declared. Do not file a post-summary correction, which is the wrong instrument and can jam the claim. Do not assume your broker filed on your behalf, since brokers file this as a billable service and generally do it only when asked.

The tape

Feb 20The Supreme Court rules 6-3 that the emergency powers act authorises no tariffs, making roughly $166 billion collected since early 2025 refundable across 330,000 importers.
Apr 20The self-service refund portal opens, covering unliquidated entries and those liquidated 80 days or less, roughly 63 percent of all entries. Declarations cannot be amended once submitted.
May 29Customs reverses course and asserts it cannot refund finally liquidated entries without a judgment naming that importer. The government appeals the universal refund order days later.
Jul 14A court filing shows about $122 billion accepted, and 9,837 approved refunds untransmitted because no bank account information has been provided.
NowEntries filed in autumn 2025 are liquidating through August and September. Each one that passes 80 days moves from a file upload to a lawsuit.

The deadline you cannot see on a calendar

There is no published claim-by date, and that is the trap rather than the reprieve. The real clock is liquidation, which happens to each entry individually about 314 days after it was filed. The portal covers entries that are unliquidated or liquidated 80 days or less. Past that, customs reversed its position in May and now says it cannot refund finally liquidated entries without a court judgment naming that importer, and the government has appealed. Entries filed in autumn 2025 are liquidating right now, through August and September. You do not miss a deadline here. You age out quietly, one entry at a time.

If you never imported anything, there is still a version of this for you. Through 2025 a great many distributors added a tariff surcharge line to invoices. That surcharge was funded by duty the importer is now collecting back with interest, and no customs rule obliges them to pass a cent of it down. It is purely contractual, which means the letter is the whole game. Pull the invoices with itemised surcharges, check your agreement for price adjustment or cost-plus language, and send a written notice asking whether they have received refunds and how they intend to credit customers. Suppliers are already fielding these.

The play

  1. Call your customs broker and ask three questions: were we the importer of record, what entries and duty do we have, and is our account enrolled for electronic refunds.
  2. Log into the customs portal and confirm bank details are on file. This single omission is why almost ten thousand approved refunds have not moved.
  3. Have the broker submit the declaration. It is a file of entry numbers, and it cannot be amended after submission, so let them check it first.
  4. If you never imported but paid tariff surcharge lines to a distributor, send the supplier letter the generator writes. There is no deadline on it and no downside to asking.

03 / The EndorsementBrief · 45 sec

The endorsement you bought for exactly this pays only if you made the phone call. Most owners bought the endorsement and skipped the call, which is how a $141,000 loss becomes uninsured.

What moved

An insurer's 2026 claims report puts business email compromise and funds transfer fraud together at 58 percent of all cyber incidents last year, with funds transfer fraud alone at 27 percent of claims and an average loss of $141,000. In August a coastal South Carolina town sent $545,000 by electronic transfer after someone impersonating a contractor's project manager asked to switch from cheque to electronic payment.

What it changes

Standard crime policies frequently do not cover this, by design. Employee theft fails because the employee is a victim rather than a thief. Computer fraud fails because a person made the decision. Funds transfer fraud fails because the instruction to the bank was genuine. The endorsement sold to close that gap carries sublimits of $25,000 to $250,000 and conditions payment on you having performed callback verification.

What not to do

Do not assume the bank absorbs it, because the transfer was authorised by a real employee and there is nothing to reverse. Do not assume your policy responds without reading the endorsement wording. Do not treat the callback as optional once you have bought the coverage, since it is the condition the insurer will test.

The tape

Mar 5An insurer's claims report finds funds transfer fraud at 27 percent of all cyber claims with an average loss of $141,000, and notes 39 percent of those events involved no confirmed email compromise at all.
Apr 14A payments fraud survey finds 76 percent of United States organisations faced attempted or actual payments fraud last year, and 48 percent of those under a billion in revenue took an actual loss.
Jun 18A federal appeals court backs an insurer denying roughly $4 million net on chief-executive impersonation transfers, on an exclusion buried in the email fraud rider.
Jul 7Construction counsel publishes the three clauses that fail on these losses, and the sublimit and verification conditions attached to the endorsement that is supposed to fix it.
Aug 5A South Carolina town sends $545,000 after an impersonator posing as a contractor's project manager asks to move from cheque to electronic payment. Liability is still unresolved.

The defense and the coverage are the same act

This is the part that changes how you think about the phone call. The endorsement most brokers sell for social engineering losses does not simply pay out when you are defrauded. It pays when you were defrauded and you had followed the verification procedure the policy specifies. One insured lost its claim precisely because it failed the procedure its own cyber policy required. So the callback is not merely the thing that would have prevented the loss. It is the condition precedent to being paid when prevention fails. Skip it and you have bought a policy that is engineered not to respond to you.

The rule fits on a card and needs no software. Any request to change where money goes gets verified by voice call to the number you already had on file before the request arrived, never a number in the email and never a reply to it. Two people know about every bank detail change. No exceptions for urgency, because urgency is the delivery mechanism. And if money does leave, the clock is the whole game: call the bank fraud line and say hold and freeze, then file at ic3.gov the same day. The federal recovery team froze $679 million last year across 3,900 incidents at a 58 percent success rate, and none of that reaches anyone who calls on Monday about a Thursday transfer.

The play

  1. Read your own endorsement this week and find two numbers: the sublimit, and the verification procedure it requires. Both are usually one page.
  2. Price the gap on the right using your largest routine vendor payment against that sublimit. The answer is what you are carrying yourself.
  3. Put the callback rule in writing and make sure two people know about any bank detail change. Written matters, because the insurer will ask what your procedure was.
  4. Save the bank fraud line in the phone of whoever cuts payments, with the words hold and freeze next to it, so nobody is searching for a number at the worst moment.

The callback is not just what stops the fraud. It is the condition your insurer will test before it pays for the fraud you did not stop.

04 / The Fifteen DaysBrief · 45 sec

Your carrier owes you the entire shipping charge on every late express package and will never mention it. You get fifteen days, and almost every guide telling you Ground counts is wrong.

What moved

Both major carriers refund 100 percent of the transportation charge on a guaranteed shipment delivered late, by any margin, including one minute. Neither volunteers it. You request it per tracking number within 15 calendar days. In April, across more than 124 million analysed shipments, one overnight service ran 54.15 percent late and another 23.83 percent.

What it changes

A shop sending fifteen next-day packages a month at around $70, with a quarter arriving late, is leaving roughly $260 a month and $3,100 a year on the table. The same 15-day channel also recovers address correction fees, residential surcharges on commercial addresses, dimensional weight errors, duplicate charges and labels billed but never shipped.

What not to do

Do not file on Ground, because the guarantee is suspended there at both carriers despite what the top search results say. Do not wait for the monthly invoice, since the window is shorter than your reconciliation cycle and that is the whole design. Do not conclude the programme is a myth after ground claims get denied, which is the trap that stops people claiming on express.

The tape

Oct 2, 2025One carrier suspends the guarantee on all United States inbound international shipments, citing the end of the de minimis exemption and a surge in formal customs clearances.
Dec 1, 2025The other carrier suspends its money back guarantee entirely during MD-11 fleet safety inspections, expecting up to $175 million in additional quarterly costs.
Feb 12Express coverage is reinstated on First, Priority and Standard Overnight, 2Day A.M. and the Extra Hours services. Everything else stays suspended, and many shippers never learned it came back.
AprAcross 124 million analysed shipments, Next Day Air Early runs 54.15 percent late, Next Day Air 23.83 percent and Standard Overnight 10.27 percent.

Why the published advice gets you denied

Search for how to claim a late delivery refund and the pages that rank will tell you Ground is covered. It is not. One carrier's own guarantee page currently lists Next Day Air Early, Next Day Air, Next Day Air Saver and 2nd Day Air A.M. and says the guarantee is suspended for everything else. The other reinstated express coverage effective February 12 this year after suspending it entirely in December during fleet groundings, and says all other services remain suspended. So a shipper follows the stale advice, files ground claims, gets rejected, decides the whole thing is folklore, and stops filing on the express packages where the hit rate runs between a quarter and a half.

The other reason nobody claims is arithmetic on their own time. It is per tracking number, not per account. There is no button that says refund everything late. Ten late packages is ten submissions, which is exactly why third party auditors exist and take a quarter to half of what they recover. If you ship enough to make that worth it, fine. If you do not, the answer is a fifteen-minute Friday habit: pull the week's guaranteed shipments, check delivery times against the commitment, and file the ones that missed. The carrier has the scan data. It knows. It simply does not say anything unless you ask.

The play

  1. Confirm which of your services are actually guaranteed by reading the carrier's own guarantee page, not a third party guide. Ground is not on either list.
  2. Set a fifteen-minute Friday habit: pull the week's guaranteed shipments, compare delivery time to commitment time, file the misses inside 15 calendar days.
  3. File through the billing centre online, or by phone. Approved credits generally post in about seven business days.
  4. Use the same window for the other recoverable items: address corrections, residential surcharges on commercial addresses, dimensional weight errors and labels billed but never shipped.

05 / The RatchetBrief · 45 sec

The fuel table on your invoice climbs on nine cents and falls on twenty-seven. That gap is not the market moving. It is the table, and it is not printed anywhere you were meant to look.

What moved

The national on-highway diesel average reached $5.454 a gallon for the week of August 17, up 19.7 cents in seven days and $1.741 on the year. Two weeks earlier the Ground surcharge table was rebuilt: the diesel pivot moved from $4.45 to $4.99, the surcharge at that pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent, and the structural floor from 20.25 to 21.25 percent.

What it changes

Above the pivot, every 9 cents of diesel adds a quarter point of surcharge. Below it, the same quarter point takes 27 cents. Three times the distance coming down. Ground parcel fuel surcharges rose 26.7 percent year on year in the first quarter while diesel itself rose 10 percent, and that 16 point spread is the shape of the table rather than the shape of the market.

What not to do

Do not quote past fourteen days without an escalator, however awkward the sentence is to add. Do not leave a delivery fee or free-shipping threshold at a number set when diesel was under four dollars. Do not assume your region tracks the national print, since California ran $6.785 the same week the Gulf Coast sat at $5.237.

The tape

Aug 10The Ground surcharge table is rebuilt. Diesel pivot moves from $4.45 to $4.99, the surcharge at that pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent, and the structural floor from 20.25 to 21.25 percent.
Aug 17The Hormuz ceasefire lapses. Diesel prints $5.454, up 19.7 cents on the week and $1.741 on the year, with United States diesel topping $100 a barrel for the first time.
Aug 19A weekly carrier fuel letter for the 19th to the 25th sets economy service at 48.00 percent, citing the $5.454 figure by name. The number is already on paper.
Aug 24Carriers reset off the prior week's print. One resets Mondays, another Wednesdays. That number is on the invoice arriving this week.

Write the term instead of absorbing the cost

The asymmetry is the whole story and almost nobody sees it, because the table is a carrier document rather than a published index. When fuel spikes it reaches your invoice within a week. When fuel falls it takes three times as far to give anything back, and in the meantime the floor underneath the whole schedule has been raised by a point. You cannot negotiate this and you cannot opt out of it. What you can do is stop being the last party in the chain who has no equivalent clause, which is the position a small operator occupies by default simply because nobody ever wrote one for them.

The clause is two sentences and it changes the shape of every quote you issue. The price holds while the national diesel average stays below a stated figure, and adjusts by a stated amount for every ten cents above it, measured against the published weekly government number so there is nothing to argue about. Set the step from your own exposure, gallons burned on the job times ten cents, rounded up. Leave the downward sentence in, because that is what makes it survive a client reading it closely. The trade-off is one slightly harder conversation at quote time, against funding somebody else's freight out of a margin you already priced thin.

The play

  1. Pull last month's carrier invoice and find the fuel surcharge as a percentage of the base rate. If nobody in the building knows that number, that is this week's finding.
  2. Generate the clause on the right and paste it into the quote template today, not into the next quote you happen to remember.
  3. Reprice the delivery fee or free-shipping threshold against diesel at $5.45 rather than against the number you set last year.
  4. Check your region against the national print before quoting work out of state. A $1.55 spread between coasts is an entire margin.
