01 / The RatchetBrief · 45 sec

Diesel hit $5.454 last week and the surcharge table was rebuilt on the 10th. Every quote you sign this week without an escalator clause eats the difference for the life of the job.

What moved

The national on-highway diesel average reached $5.454 a gallon for the week of August 17, up 19.7 cents in seven days and $1.741 on the year, after the Strait of Hormuz ceasefire lapsed. Two weeks earlier the Ground surcharge table was rebuilt: the diesel pivot moved from $4.45 to $4.99, the surcharge at that pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent.

What it changes

Above the new pivot, every 9 cents of diesel adds a quarter point of surcharge. Below it, the same quarter point takes 27 cents. Fuel reaches your invoice in days and leaves it over months. Carriers reset weekly off the prior week's print, so last Monday's number is on this week's bill, and any fixed price you quote now carries it until the job closes.

What not to do

Do not quote past fourteen days without an escalator, however awkward the sentence feels to add. Do not leave a delivery fee or free-shipping threshold at a number you chose when diesel was under four dollars. Do not assume your region tracks the national print, because California ran $6.785 the same week the Gulf Coast sat at $5.237.

The tape

Aug 10The Ground surcharge table is rebuilt. Diesel pivot moves from $4.45 to $4.99, surcharge at pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent, structural floor from 20.25 to 21.25 percent.
Aug 17The Hormuz ceasefire lapses. Diesel prints $5.454, up 19.7 cents on the week and $1.741 on the year, with United States diesel topping $100 a barrel for the first time.
Aug 19A weekly carrier fuel letter for the 19th to the 25th sets economy service at 48.00 percent, citing the $5.454 figure by name. The number is already on paper.
Aug 20Gasoline reaches $4.1044 against $3.1337 a year earlier, the highest ever recorded on that date, with crude settling at $85.83.
Aug 24Carriers reset off last week's print. One resets Mondays, another Wednesdays. The $5.454 number is on the invoice you receive this week.

The table is asymmetric on purpose

Here is the part that costs people money. The surcharge table is not symmetrical, and the gap is where your margin goes. Above the $4.99 pivot it takes 9 cents of diesel movement to add a quarter point of surcharge. Below it, that same quarter point requires 27 cents. Three times the distance coming down as going up. Ground parcel fuel surcharges rose 26.7 percent year on year in the first quarter while diesel itself rose 10 percent. That 16-point spread is not a market. It is the shape of the table doing exactly what it was built to do.

So stop treating fuel as a cost you absorb and start treating it as a term you write. The clause is two sentences. The price holds while the national diesel average stays below a stated figure, and adjusts by a stated amount for every ten cents above it, measured against the published weekly number so nobody argues about the source. The trade-off is real and worth naming: you will have one slightly harder conversation at quote time. The alternative is funding somebody else's freight out of a margin you already priced thin.

The play

  1. Pull last month's carrier invoice and find the fuel surcharge as a percentage of base rate. If nobody in the building knows that number by heart, that is this week's finding.
  2. Generate the escalator clause on the right and paste it into your quote template today, not into the next quote you happen to remember.
  3. Reprice the delivery fee or free-shipping threshold against diesel at $5.45. The number you set last year was priced against a different world.
  4. Check your region against the national print before you quote work out of state. A $1.55 spread between California and the Gulf Coast is a whole margin.

02 / The DateBrief · 45 sec

Extended partnership and S-corp returns are due September 15, and the penalty is charged per partner, per month. A four-partner LLC that slips to November owes about $3,060 before interest starts.

What moved

The IRS confirmed on August 21 that interest rates hold for the fourth quarter, keeping individual underpayments at 7 percent compounded daily from October 1. That confirmation lands three weeks before September 15, the hard deadline for extended Form 1065 and Form 1120-S returns and for the third quarter estimated tax installment.

What it changes

The late-filing penalty on those two forms is not a flat fee. It runs $255 per partner or shareholder per month for returns due to be filed this year. Four partners, three months late, is $3,060 in penalty alone, before the 7 percent daily compounding touches anything unpaid. A nine-person shop with three owners is exposed the same way a large firm is, without the staff to catch it.

What not to do

Do not assume your preparer has the return because you filed the extension in March. Do not skip the third quarter estimate on the theory that you will settle everything in April, since the safe harbours exist and missing them is expensive. Do not confuse the September 15 date with October 15, which is the extended personal and corporate deadline, not this one.

The tape

Mar 2026The extension was filed, which moved the deadline and nothing else. It did not extend the time to pay, and the six-month clock started running that day.
Aug 21The IRS confirms fourth quarter rates hold. Individual underpayments stay at 7 percent compounded daily, effective October 1, alongside 6 percent corporate overpayment and 9 percent large corporate underpayment.
Sep 15Extended Form 1065 and Form 1120-S returns are due. The third quarter estimated tax installment is due the same day. The per-partner penalty starts accruing on the 16th.
Sep 30 and Oct 15Extended trust returns land on the 30th, extended personal and corporate returns on the 15th. Filing one of those on time does not cure a missed September deadline.

Why the per-partner structure catches people

Most owners carry one mental model of a late-filing penalty: a flat charge, annoying, survivable. These two forms do not work that way, and the difference is structural. Section 6698 and Section 6699 charge per partner or per shareholder, per month or part of a month. The clock does not care whether you owe tax. A partnership that owes nothing at all still accrues the penalty simply because the return is late, and the amount scales with how many owners the business has, which means the businesses most likely to lose track of the date are also the ones with several people who each assumed somebody else was handling it.

The move this week is a five-minute email, not a project. Ask your preparer to confirm in writing that they have everything they need for the return and that they will file by the 15th. Ask specifically what is still outstanding on their side. If the answer is vague, that is your answer, and you have three weeks rather than three days. Then pay the third quarter estimate. The safe harbours are plain: owe under $1,000, or pay 90 percent of this year, or 100 percent of last year. Prior year is the one you can calculate today without knowing how the year ends.

The play

  1. Email your preparer today and ask two questions in writing: do you have everything for the return, and will you file by September 15. Vague answers count as no.
  2. Price your own exposure on the right using your actual number of partners or shareholders, then decide how hard to push on that email.
  3. Pay the third quarter estimate on the 15th using the prior-year safe harbour, which you can calculate now without knowing how the year finishes.
  4. Put October 15 in the calendar separately for the personal return. Two different deadlines, a month apart, and people routinely merge them into one.

03 / The CliffBrief · 45 sec

El Salvador work permits stop counting on September 9 and Ukraine on October 19. The fix is a folder, a pen and a calendar entry, done this week rather than on the 8th.

What moved

Temporary Protected Status for El Salvador and every benefit attached to it terminates September 9. Ukraine terminates October 19. Immigration services auto-extended a named list of already-expired permits up to those dates and no further: eight specific expiration dates for El Salvador, two for Ukraine. Employer guidance landed across August 18 to 21 telling employers to write the new dates into existing forms now.

What it changes

Roughly 152,000 Salvadoran permit holders are in the workforce, concentrated in construction, maintenance, transportation, manufacturing and food service. That is your payroll, not somebody else's. Paperwork violations run $288 to $2,861 per form. Continuing to employ someone past expiry runs $716 to $5,724 per worker on a first offence, rising to $28,619 by the third.

What not to do

Do not sort the binder by name or by country, which is discrimination and builds a second, larger problem on top of the first. Do not terminate anyone early on an assumption, because authorisation runs until it actually expires. Do not leave the reverification date in one person's head, since September 9 is sixteen days out and payroll will be busy on the 8th.

The tape

Jun 25The Supreme Court decides Mullin v. Doe, clearing the path for these terminations to run on schedule rather than stalling in litigation.
Aug 12The El Salvador alert confirms the designation and related benefits terminate September 9, and names the eight permit expiration dates the auto-extension covers.
Aug 14The Ukraine alert follows, extending permits that expired April 19, 2025 and October 19, 2023 through October 19, 2026 and no further.
Aug 18 to 21Employer guidance lands from several firms, all saying the same thing: write the new dates in now, audit by document category, do not audit by nationality.
Sep 9El Salvador terminates. From that date, continuing to employ an affected worker without new documentation is a knowing-employment violation rather than a paperwork one.

Audit by document, never by person

The method matters more than the deadline here, because the wrong method creates the more expensive problem. You sort the binder by permit category code, A-12 and C-19, and you look at expiration dates printed on documents. You do not sort by name, you do not sort by country of birth, and you do not ask a subset of staff about their status because you have a hunch. A document-based audit of every file is defensible. A targeted one is a discrimination claim that outlives the deadline that prompted it, and costs considerably more than the penalty you were trying to avoid.

Then have the conversation early. A worker whose authorisation lapses cannot stay on payroll, and pretending otherwise converts a staffing problem into a knowing-employment violation with a per-worker price attached. Tell affected people this week which date applies to them and what documentation would change it, so they have a fortnight to act instead of a weekend. You may lose the same person either way. What you keep is the relationship, a clean record, and the chance that they solve it before the date rather than after. Sixteen days is enough time for someone to file something. Two days is not.

The play

  1. Whoever keeps the binder pulls every form this week and sorts by permit category code. Every file gets looked at, not a selected few.
  2. Write the new expiration date into the expiration field on each affected form and reference the immigration alert in the additional information box.
  3. Generate the tracking sheet and the employee notice on the right, then hand the notice to affected staff this week rather than in September.
  4. Put every reverification date into a shared calendar with a named owner, so the deadline does not live in one person's memory through a busy payroll fortnight.

04 / The BasketBrief · 45 sec

Both giants reported the same quarter: customers still came, they just bought the same amount. Twenty-one thousand price cuts between them says the fight moved to basket size.

What moved

Target reported on August 19 with comparable sales up 3.8 percent, transactions up 3.6 percent and average ticket up 0.2 percent. Walmart reported on the 20th: United States comparable sales up 2.6 percent excluding fuel, transactions up 1.5 percent, ticket up 1.1 percent. Target cut prices on more than 10,000 items, Walmart expanded rollbacks past 11,000.

What it changes

Read the split rather than the headline. Nearly all of Target's growth came from more visits, almost none from bigger baskets. That is a customer who is still willing to come to you and unwilling to spend more once inside. Walmart's own club business showed it plainly: visits up 7 percent, spend per visit down 2.5 percent. The lever that moves is what you attach to a trip that is already happening.

What not to do

Do not read this as demand collapsing and start cutting hours, because footfall held. Do not answer it with a broad discount, since the two largest retailers just cut 21,000 prices and you cannot win that. Do not add a loyalty scheme this month instead, which is a Q1 project being used to avoid a Q3 decision.

The tape

Aug 19Target reports comps up 3.8 percent on transactions up 3.6 and ticket up 0.2, with digital up 8.7 percent and same-day delivery growing more than 25 percent.
Aug 19Target confirms it cut prices on more than 10,000 frequently purchased items during the quarter, its answer to a customer counting at the shelf.
Aug 20Walmart reports United States comps up 2.6 percent excluding fuel, the slowest pace in six years, missing a 3.8 percent forecast. Rollbacks expand past 11,000 items from roughly 7,200.
Aug 20The club business separates the signal from the noise: visits up 7 percent, spending per visit down 2.5 percent. Same customer, smaller basket.

Traffic and ticket are two different businesses

When someone tells you comparable sales rose 3.8 percent, they have told you almost nothing. The number that matters is what produced it. Target's came from 3.6 points of transactions and 0.2 points of ticket, which means the growth is entirely people walking in and essentially none of it is people spending more. Walmart's slowest comparable growth in six years came with transactions still positive. Its chief financial officer pointed at diesel above four dollars and called it a psychological threshold. Customers are not gone. They are counting, and they are counting at the moment of the second item, not the first.

That points the work somewhere specific. Do not touch your entry price, which is what everyone reaches for and what the largest players have already commoditised. Work the attachment instead: the one item, service or size that logically follows what they already chose, offered at the counter or the cart by a person or a line of text rather than a campaign. One more dollar on a ticket you were already going to ring is worth more than one more customer, because it carries no acquisition cost. The trade-off is that it requires your staff to actually say the sentence, which is a training problem, not a marketing one.

The play

  1. Pull your own last four weeks and split it the way they did: transactions and average ticket, separately. Most operators only ever look at the total.
  2. Pick one attachment that logically follows your most common purchase. One, not a menu of them, and price it so the yes is easy.
  3. Write the sentence your staff will actually say and put it where they can see it. An untrained attach offer is a discount you gave away for nothing.
  4. Run the calculator on the right to see what a single dollar of attachment is worth across a year before you decide it is too small to bother with.

05 / The PatchBrief · 45 sec

A macOS screen sharing flaw handing out root without a password went on the actively-exploited list last Tuesday. Around 40,000 machines are reachable, and the fix is free.

What moved

On August 18 a macOS Screen Sharing vulnerability was added to the federal actively-exploited catalogue with a three-day remediation deadline. It scores 9.8 and gives root without any credentials through a flaw in the remote password implementation. It was patched on August 6. Its severity was raised from 7.1 to 9.8 on the 14th and flagged automatable.

What it changes

Roughly 40,000 machines are reachable from the internet on the screen sharing port, about half of them in the United States. Attackers are taking root and installing crypto miners. Any small office that switched screen sharing on once for remote support and never switched it off is in that population. Root access means full disk, saved credentials and a foothold that survives a reboot.

What not to do

Do not assume this is handled because your machines update themselves, since vulnerable builds run right up to the version before the patch. Do not leave screen sharing on because someone might need it later, which is how it got left on the first time. Do not skip the router rule on the theory that you are behind a firewall without checking that you are.

The tape

Aug 6Apple patches the flaw in macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9 and Sonoma 14.8.9. Every build below those remains vulnerable.
Aug 7 and 12A national cyber centre issues an advisory, then updates it five days later after active exploitation is observed in the wild.
Aug 14Severity is raised from 7.1 to 9.8 and the flaw is flagged automatable, meaning it can be run at scale against anything reachable on the port.
Aug 18It is added to the actively-exploited catalogue with a three-day federal deadline, and limited exploitation is confirmed, with root sign-ins observed through screen sharing.

The three-day clock is not yours, and that matters

The August 21 deadline attached to this listing binds federal agencies, not your business, and plenty of coverage blurs that. Here is what the listing actually tells you, which is more useful than a deadline you do not owe: the government has confirmed this is being exploited right now, in the wild, against real machines. That is a different category from a vulnerability that merely exists. The severity was raised from 7.1 to 9.8 eight days after the patch shipped, which is what happens when researchers discover the thing is easier to weaponise than first thought, and it was marked automatable, meaning it can be run at scale against anything reachable.

The work is fifteen minutes across the whole office and needs nobody technical. Update every Mac to the patched build, turn screen sharing off in Sharing settings, and block the port at the router. Do all three rather than picking one, because updating without switching off the service leaves the service exposed to whatever comes next, and switching off without updating leaves the build vulnerable to anyone who gets inside another way. The trade-off worth stating plainly: if a machine was already rooted, patching does not undo it, and the correct response at that point is a wipe and reinstall rather than a cleanup.

The play

  1. Update every Mac in the building to 26.6.1, 15.7.9 or 14.8.9 depending on which system it runs. Check each machine rather than assuming automatic updates ran.
  2. Open Sharing settings on every machine and turn screen sharing off. If somebody needs remote support later, it takes ten seconds to turn back on.
  3. Block the screen sharing port at the router. Being behind a firewall is a thing to verify this week, not a thing to assume.
  4. Use the checklist on the right so the person doing this can tick machines off, and so you have a record of which ones were actually done.
