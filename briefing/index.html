01 / The NumberBrief · 50 sec

Payrolls went negative in July. Price both Septembers before the Fed picks one.

What moved

The July jobs report landed Friday: the economy shed 23,000 jobs against forecasts of an 80,000 gain, and May and June were revised down a combined 103,000. Unemployment ticked down to 4.1 percent, and annual wage growth slowed to 3.2 percent. Markets repriced within the hour: the quarter-point rate increase that had been the September base case became the less likely outcome, with the Fed's decision due September 16.

What it changes

Two operator facts moved at once. Hiring: the market loosened, retail cut 19,400 jobs and private payrolls barely grew, so candidates who were unreachable in spring answer the phone now, and wage pressure is easing. Borrowing: the increase lenders were pricing into September lost its majority, which steadies every floating-rate credit line, SBA loan, and equipment note you carry. Cheaper money is not here; more expensive money got less likely.

What not to do

Do not read one negative month as a recession and freeze hiring you already budgeted; a loose market is exactly when good people become reachable. Do not rush to lock a fixed rate out of fear this week, because the pressure pushing rates up just eased. And do not let September 16 pass unwatched: if you carry floating-rate debt, that afternoon prices it either way, and you should know your exposure to the quarter point before the announcement.

The tape

Aug 7The Bureau of Labor Statistics reports July payrolls at minus 23,000 against expectations near plus 80,000. Unemployment falls to 4.1 percent. Average annual earnings growth slows to 3.2 percent, under the 3.5 percent estimate.
RevisionsMay drops from 129,000 to 63,000 and June from 57,000 to 20,000, a combined 103,000 fewer jobs than previously reported. Against expectations, the total July shortfall runs roughly a quarter million positions.
SectorsPrivate payrolls add 30,000 against 78,000 expected. Government sheds 53,000, retail 19,400, financial activities 14,000. Healthcare adds 22,000 and manufacturing 5,000. The customer-facing economy is where the cutting is.
Sept 16The fed funds range sits at 3.50 to 3.75 percent. Before the report, futures priced a 55 percent chance of a quarter-point increase at the September meeting; after it, the odds flipped to 56 percent for holding steady. Not a cut. A reprieve.

A loose market is a quiet opening

The instinct on a bad jobs number is defensive, and for hiring it should be the opposite. The roles you could not fill in March, when every candidate had three offers, are fillable in a market that just shed retail and office jobs by the tens of thousands. Wage growth at 3.2 percent means the counter-offer math has moved in your favor for the first time in years. None of that requires a prediction about recessions; it only requires noticing who answers the ad this month versus five months ago.

The borrowing side is about knowing one number. A quarter point on your floating-rate debt is a specific dollar figure, and most owners have never written it down; they carry the anxiety instead of the arithmetic. Price it once with the instrument on the right and September 16 becomes a calendar entry instead of a cliff. If the Fed holds, nothing changes. If it moves, you already knew the cost to the dollar.

The play

  1. Reopen the one or two roles you shelved in spring. Run the ads for two weeks and count who answers now; the market did the recruiting-budget work for you.
  2. List every floating-rate obligation: the credit line, the SBA 7(a), equipment notes, any card balance that rides. Mark which ones reprice with prime.
  3. Run the list through the instrument on the right so the quarter point is a dollar figure, not a worry. Put September 16 on the calendar with the number attached.
  4. Price planned raises against the 3.2 percent tape, not against January's market. Pay well on purpose, not out of stale fear.

02 / The RepriceBrief · 45 sec

QuickBooks raised its prices again August 1. Audit the tier before you renew it.

What moved

Intuit repriced QuickBooks Online on August 1, the second increase this year. Simple Start now lists at $38 a month, Essentials at $85, Plus at $140, and Advanced at $340, with trackers putting the upper tiers 40 to 70 percent above where they started the year. The new prices reach existing customers at renewal. One offset: Advanced now bundles Workforce Elite payroll, which used to be a separate subscription.

What it changes

Bookkeeping software stopped being a rounding error: Plus is now $1,680 a year and Advanced is $4,080 before add-ons. Two increases in a single year is the vendor telling you how it plans to behave, so the renewal stops being automatic and becomes a tier-fit question. Most businesses on Plus use one feature that keeps them there, usually inventory or project tracking; most businesses on Advanced were sold headroom, not features. A downshift is a ten-minute change worth up to four figures a year.

What not to do

Do not absorb the increase without opening the feature list; you pay for the tier, not for your usage of it. Do not migrate accounting software in anger in August if your books close in December, because switching costs are real and timing matters more than the vendor. And do not judge the Advanced price without netting the payroll bundle; depending on your payroll tier and headcount, the new $340 can come out close to a wash.

The tape

Aug 1The new list prices take effect: Simple Start $38, Essentials $85, Plus $140, Advanced $340 a month. They apply to new subscribers after the introductory window and to existing customers at their next renewal. Intuit's own pricing page already reads the new numbers.
The yearThis is the second 2026 repricing; the tiers already stepped up in the spring. Third-party trackers put Essentials and Plus roughly 40 percent, and Advanced roughly 70 percent, above their January levels. Intuit points to its AI buildout as the driver.
The offsetAdvanced now includes Workforce Elite payroll at no extra charge. For a business already paying for a mid-tier payroll subscription, the bundled price can land near neutral; for everyone else the increase is the increase.
ContextIssue No. 003 covered the other side of this trade: the wholesale price of the intelligence inside these tools keeps falling while the subscription prices on top of it keep rising. No vendor reprices downward voluntarily. Renewal is where you vote.

Pay for the tier you use

The tier audit is one plain sentence per feature. Write down what actually keeps you on your plan: if you are on Plus, is it inventory, project profitability, or budgets, and do you open them monthly or did you open them once in onboarding? If you are on Advanced, name the thing Essentials or Plus cannot do that you did last quarter. If the sentence comes out empty, the downshift is free money: Plus to Essentials keeps $660 a year, Advanced to Plus keeps $2,400.

If the audit says leave entirely, leave on a schedule instead of a mood. Accounting migrations succeed at fiscal year close with two months of parallel running, and they fail in August with none. Between now and then, export monthly backups and price the alternatives calmly; a rushed migration costs more than a year of the increase you are fleeing.

The play

  1. Pull your current plan and last invoice, and write this year's new annual number next to last year's. The instrument on the right does the arithmetic.
  2. Run the tier audit: one sentence per feature that keeps you on the plan. Empty sentence, lower tier. Downshifting at renewal takes minutes inside account settings.
  3. On Advanced: price your payroll both ways before judging the $340. Current payroll subscription plus a lower tier, against Advanced with Workforce Elite bundled.
  4. If you decide to switch vendors, set the date at your fiscal year close, start monthly exports now, and run the two systems in parallel for one clean close before cutting over.

03 / The RefundBrief · 45 sec

Washington has returned $86 billion in tariff refunds. Check whether some of it is yours.

What moved

The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs in February, and the refund machine built since has now moved $86.3 billion back to importers, $49 billion of it in June alone. Customs opened the third and final phase in late July for the hardest cases, entries already finally liquidated, under a July 15 court order. A progress report landed August 4 and a settlement conference ran August 5. Nearly 10,000 processed refunds sit undelivered because importers never supplied bank details.

What it changes

If you imported anything between spring 2025 and this February, components, packaging, finished goods, you may be owed duty money with interest, and nobody will chase you to take it. The refund is not automatic in every case: recent entries flowed through the first two phases, but the oldest, finally liquidated entries currently require having filed at the Court of International Trade, and that door favors the people who act now. Your customs broker can pull your entry history in an afternoon; that report is the whole first step.

What not to do

Do not assume your freight forwarder or broker filed claims on your behalf; refund filings were never part of the standard service. Do not ignore small entries, because duties plus interest across a year of shipments add up and the first step costs one email. And do not confuse the refund with relief; money coming back on 2025's duties says nothing about what you pay on next month's containers, which is the subject of the next article.

The tape

Feb 20The Supreme Court rules 6 to 3 that the across-the-board IEEPA tariffs exceeded the statute. Customs stands up the CAPE platform to process more than $100 billion in refunds of invalidated duties.
Apr to JunPhase one covers unliquidated and recent entries; phase two takes the complex reconciliation claims. By late July, $86.3 billion in duties and interest has been transmitted, with $49 billion moving in June alone.
Jul 15The Court of International Trade orders Customs to reliquidate certain finally liquidated entries across roughly 3,700 pending cases, opening phase three. The unresolved question: whether importers who never filed suit get the same treatment. Trade counsel are telling non-litigants to file now and preserve the claim.
Aug 4CBP files its progress report; a settlement conference follows on the 5th. Inside the numbers: 9,837 processed refunds parked because the importer never submitted ACH banking information. Money, approved, waiting on a form.

One email starts the claim

The reason most owed businesses will never collect is not eligibility, it is that nobody owns the question. The duties were paid eighteen months ago, buried inside landed costs and passed along in prices, and the person who signed the customs forms was a broker you hear from twice a year. Reversing that takes one instruction: ask the broker for a report of every entry from March 2025 through February 2026 with duties paid under IEEPA authority flagged, with totals. The instrument on the right writes the email.

What comes back sorts itself into three piles. Entries refunded already: reconcile the deposit. Entries in phases one or two: confirm the claim exists and your banking details are on file, because ten thousand approved refunds are sitting on that step. Finally liquidated entries: if the total justifies it, put a trade attorney on a fixed fee and decide about a CIT filing while the door is open. Five figures owed is worth an afternoon of counsel; three figures is worth the broker email and nothing more.

The play

  1. Send the broker email today: all entries March 2025 through February 2026, IEEPA duties flagged, totals by entry status. The tool writes it.
  2. Match the refunded entries against your bank statements; interest rides along and the deposits do not announce themselves clearly.
  3. Submit ACH details the moment any claim exists. Approved money waits on that form and on nothing else.
  4. If finally liquidated entries hold five figures, get a trade attorney's read on a CIT filing now, while phase three's terms are being set, not after they close.

04 / The DeadlineBrief · 45 sec

A 50 percent tariff on Canadian goods lands August 19. Reprice before it reaches your invoices.

What moved

The White House invoked Section 338 of the 1930 Tariff Act for the first time in the statute's history: a 50 percent duty on roughly $20 billion of Canadian goods, cement and wine among them, effective midnight Eastern on August 19. Lumber escapes this one only because it is already taxed elsewhere; Canadian softwood carries about 35 percent in combined duties, and framing lumber is up more than 30 percent since December. The homebuilders put the tariff cost of a new American home at ten thousand dollars.

What it changes

If your inputs touch Canada, cement, aggregates, food and drink, packaging, components, your landed costs step up in nine days, and the quote you gave three weeks ago may already be underwater. This is a repricing problem before it is a sourcing problem: bids need expiry dates, new contracts need a tariff pass-through line, and signed jobs priced on spring materials need a reread before you order against them. Suppliers will fold their own increases into this window, and the invoice will not tell you which line moved.

What not to do

Do not eat a 50 percent input increase to protect a handshake price; reopen the quote and show the customer the tariff line, because they are reading the same headlines you are. Do not stockpile more than you can store and finance to beat one date; this duty is a negotiating position in the USMCA talks and can move again in either direction. And do not take a supplier's word on origin, because the duty follows the country of origin, not the warehouse the truck left.

The tape

Jul 21The proclamations issue: Section 338, dormant since 1930, gets its first use. Fifty percent duties on selected Canadian products, framed openly as pressure in the USMCA renegotiation. Trade lawyers call it an opening bid.
Aug 19The duties take effect at midnight Eastern on roughly $20 billion in goods: cement, wine, and a list built around products the administration ties to Canadian treatment of American cars, alcohol, and dairy.
The stackArticles already covered by Section 232 are exempt, which spares lumber the new 50 but leaves softwood at roughly 35.2 percent combined. Framing lumber is up more than 30 percent from December lows and softwood imports are down 28 percent year over year. The exemption is relief on paper only.
ContextThis lands on top of the new Section 301 baseline of 10 to 12.5 percent announced in late July across dozens of trading partners. The refund story in the previous article is the old regime being unwound; this is the new one being built. Two ledgers, both yours.

Nine days is a repricing window

The businesses that get hurt by tariff moves are rarely the ones paying the duty at the dock; they are the ones holding fixed-price commitments made against costs that no longer exist. A bid issued in July against August 19 materials is a donation. The defensive work is clerical and fast: every open quote gets an expiry date, every new contract gets a pass-through line, and every signed job with affected inputs gets priced for exposure so the decision, absorb, split, or reopen, is made deliberately per job instead of discovered at closeout.

Customers accept repricing they can see the cause of. A line item that says what the tariff did to the inputs converts better than a silent 8 percent bump, and it positions you as the operator who reads the tape rather than the vendor who pads. The one-line clause for everything you issue from today: prices reflect duties in effect at issue; changes in tariff or duty rates flow through at cost, documented.

The play

  1. Pull your top 20 purchased items and mark anything of Canadian origin or from a Canadian supplier. Ask for origin confirmation in writing; the duty follows origin, not the shipping address.
  2. Reprice open quotes and standing bids that carry affected inputs. Add an expiry date and the pass-through clause to everything issued from today.
  3. Run signed jobs through the instrument on the right so each one has an exposure number and a decision: absorb, split, or reopen.
  4. Buy ahead only what storage and cash comfortably allow through September. Hedging a negotiating position with your working capital is a trade you do not need.

05 / The FilingBrief · 45 sec

Insurers want 14 percent more for small-group health cover next year. Start the renewal now.

What moved

KFF published its read of the 2027 rate filings this week: nearly 300 insurers selling small-group coverage across all fifty states are asking for a median 14 percent increase, with 59 percent of them between 10 and 20 percent and six carriers above 30. The filings cite a 10.8 percent underlying medical cost trend, GLP-1 drug spend, specialty biologics, and behavioral health growing more than 20 percent a year. Regulators approve final rates through the fall; the new prices land January 1.

What it changes

Fourteen percent on a six-figure annual premium bill is a five-figure increase, stacked on last year's climb. The filings also explain the quote before it arrives: the fully insured small-group pool has shrunk from 17 million covered lives in 2013 to 10 million, and every healthy group that exits to level-funded raises the price on whoever stays. That is the decision on your desk this fall, stay in the pool, restructure the plan, or change how you contribute, and groups that start in August get quoted options that groups opening the envelope in November do not.

What not to do

Do not wait for the renewal packet; by the time it arrives, alternatives cannot be quoted and implemented before the deadline. Do not jump to level-funded on price alone, because a bad claims year comes back to you; read the stop-loss terms before you sign, not after. And do not cut the benefit silently to absorb the cost. In the labor market article one describes, the health plan is part of the wage, and staff price it that way even when owners do not.

The tape

Aug 6The Peterson-KFF Health System Tracker publishes the analysis: 295 small-group insurers, median proposed increase 14 percent for 2027, the middle half of requests running 10 to 18 percent. Six insurers ask for more than 30.
DriversThe filings themselves name the causes: underlying medical costs trending at 10.8 percent, GLP-1 utilization, new biologics without generic competition, behavioral health claims growing north of 20 percent annually, and dispute-resolution costs from the No Surprises Act adding almost a point at some carriers.
The poolFully insured small-group enrollment has fallen from 17 million people in 2013 to 10 million. Healthier groups keep leaving for level-funded and self-insured arrangements, which concentrates risk on the groups that remain and feeds the next year's increase. The spiral is structural, not cyclical.
The calendarFilings run May through August, state approvals land October and November, rates go live January 1. Approved rates historically come in near or slightly under the requests. The window where a business can actually shop closes when the approvals publish and brokers flood.

August is when the renewal is a negotiation

The renewal packet is designed to be accepted: it arrives late, quotes one option, and prices delay as the default. The counter is to run the process backward. Know your exposure number now, put your broker on notice this month, and require three quotes by October 1: the current plan renewed, one plan-design variation, and a level-funded option with the stop-loss terms attached in writing. Brokers produce alternatives for the clients who ask in August and renewals for the ones who call in November.

Separate the plan decision from the contribution decision. A defined-dollar contribution, the business pays a fixed amount per employee and employees choose their trade-off across two plan options, converts an open-ended 14 percent liability into a budgeted line you control, without a silent benefit cut. It also puts the real conversation on the table: here is what we pay, here are the choices, both said out loud before January instead of discovered in a paycheck.

The play

  1. Put your current premium and headcount into the instrument on the right; hold your own number before the packet holds it for you.
  2. Email the broker this week: three quotes by October 1, renewal, one design change, one level-funded with stop-loss terms in writing.
  3. Decide the contribution structure separately from the plan pick; defined-dollar keeps the budget fixed while employees keep the choice.
  4. Whatever you choose, present it with the compensation picture, not as a benefits memo. Article one says who is reachable in this market; the plan is part of why they stay.
