01 / Works nowBrief · 45 sec

Owners using AI are cutting outside vendors. Marketing gets cut most.

What moved

On September 29 Citizens released its Q4 Business Pulse: 500 leaders surveyed September 1 to 17, weighted to the U.S. small business population. Of firms using AI regularly across several jobs, 94% cut or ended at least one outside service. Marketing led at 60%, then data and reporting at 39% and bookkeeping at 38%. On October 22 Goldman Sachs reported 42% of its AI users run financial analysis on it, up from 18% in 2024.

What it changes

The first AI savings are landing on vendor invoices, not payroll. Goldman found 84% of AI users changed no headcount. The money comes off the monthly retainer: the posts, the report, the categorizing. Those jobs repeat on a schedule and come back as drafts someone reviews. Your bar is simple. A $2,000 monthly retainer buys 50 staff hours at $40 an hour. If your team does the job in less, checking included, you keep the difference.

What not to do

Do not fire the agency before a 60-day side by side run proves your team ships the same work on time. Do not read the 94% as proof AI caused the cuts; it is a survey of heavy users, and the data does not show whether the work moved in house. Do not start with bookkeeping; a bad post costs a day, a bad ledger costs a tax season. Do not cut the person who checks the output.

The tape

Sep 29Citizens releases its Q4 2026 Business Pulse, 500 leaders weighted to the small business population. 94% of multi-job AI users cut at least one outside service: marketing 60%, data and reporting 39%, bookkeeping 38%.
Oct 8TechInformed reads the Citizens numbers and flags the gap. The survey does not show whether AI replaced the provider, whether work moved in house, or whether "reduced" means a cancelled contract or a smaller one.
Oct 20Google adds a business page to the Gemini app. It connects Google Business Profile, Google Ads and Workspace, plus Canva, Dropbox and Shopify, and builds on-brand visuals from your logo and colors. Rolling out to all users, no price named.
Oct 22Goldman Sachs publishes its 10,000 Small Businesses AI report, 2,473 owners surveyed by Babson in August. 86% use AI. Financial analysis use rose from 18% to 42% of users since 2024. Only 18% of users have fully built it in.
NextCitizens runs Business Pulse every quarter, so the next read comes with the new year. Your own invoices are the better data: one job, 60 days in house, hours counted, before any contract changes.

The retainer is repeat work

Outside vendors sell repeat work by the month: twelve posts, one dashboard, a categorized ledger. That is the shape AI handles best, a known template filled with this month's facts. Goldman's data shows owners moving past writing into the numbers. Written marketing fell from 74% to 65% of uses while financial analysis rose from 18% to 42%. The part most owners miss is the edge of the job. The vendor also carried judgment: which post runs, which number looks off, who calls the bank. AI drafts the work. It does not own the result, and only 18% of Goldman's AI users have fully built it in.

Pull twelve months of vendor payments and list every service billed monthly. Pick the one with the most repeat work and the least risk; for most owners that is social posts or the monthly report. Run it in house for 60 days beside the vendor, on the AI you already pay for: ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot or Gemini, which now connects to Google Business Profile and Google Ads. Time every hour, checking included. If those hours at your cost plus the seat beat the retainer, cut the scope before you cancel. The trade-off is attention. The vendor's hours become your manager's hours, and somebody has to own them.

The play

  1. This week, you or the office manager: export twelve months of vendor payments from QuickBooks or Xero. List every outside service billed monthly, with the amount.
  2. Circle one job with repeat work and low risk. Social posts or the monthly report go first. Payroll, sales tax and the ledger stay with the pros for now.
  3. Paste into ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini: "Here are the last three months of work our agency delivered: [paste]. List every repeating task, how often it ships and the facts each one needs. Then write one reusable prompt per task that produces the same deliverable from this month's facts."
  4. Run it beside the vendor for 60 days. Same deadlines, same deliverables, nothing of yours ships. Your team logs every hour, checking included.
  5. Use The Retainer Review on this page to write the 60-day plan, the bar the test has to clear and the scope note to your vendor.
  6. On day 60, compare your hours at cost plus the AI seat against the retainer. Cut the scope, keep the vendor on the judgment work, or keep everything as is.
Sources

Agencies and bookkeepers bill by the month for repeat work, and repeat work from a template is the job AI drafts fastest for a person to check.

02 / BewareBrief · 45 sec

AI can fake a voice you trust. Call the number on file.

What moved

On September 25 Reuters reported that Fideuram, the private bank of Intesa Sanpaolo, sent about €95 million overseas after a WhatsApp message from a fake CEO and a confirming call in a lawyer's AI-cloned voice. About €53 million is back. In April the FBI's 2025 crime report counted 22,364 complaints that mentioned AI and $893 million in losses, and said criminals use voice cloning to request wire payments.

What it changes

The phone call was your safety check. A cloned voice turns it into the scam's closing step. The insurance side is worse than most owners assume. Many cyber and crime policies cap fraudulent transfer losses at $250,000 or less, and some only pay if staff confirmed the bank change on a separate call. A Texas builder lost $874,863 to a fake steel vendor. The insurer paid $250,000 and the court said that was all.

What not to do

Do not call the number in the email, the text or the invoice. The Texas builder did exactly that and reached the fraudster. Do not let one person change a payee's bank details and also send the first payment. Do not treat a familiar voice or a face on video as proof; urgency and secrecy are the tell. Do not assume your cyber policy covers a payment your own staff sent. Find the social engineering limit this week.

The tape

FebFideuram's then chairman gets a WhatsApp message that appears to come from Intesa Sanpaolo's CEO, asking for urgent help with an overseas deal. A follow-up call in a lawyer's AI-cloned voice confirms it. Transfers go mainly to China and Hong Kong.
Mar 9A federal court in Texas rules for the insurer in Perry and Perry Builders v. Cowbell. A fake steel vendor took $874,863.70 in two ACH payments a minute apart. The policy's cyber crime limit was $250,000, and that is all it paid.
AprThe FBI's 2025 Internet Crime Report counts 22,364 complaints mentioning AI and $893 million in losses. Business email compromise with an AI component cost over $30 million. The report says criminals use voice cloning to request wire payments.
Jun 22Insurance Journal reports that cyber and crime policies often cap fraudulent transfer losses at $250,000 or less, that many standard cyber policies exclude social engineering, and that some require a separate confirming call before a bank change.
Sep 25Reuters reports the Fideuram loss at about €95 million. Roughly €53 million is recovered; about €36 million moved into crypto and is untraced. Milan prosecutors are investigating one foreign national. Both banks decline to comment.

The phone call is the target

Payment fraud runs on one trick: get a real employee to send real money. For years the defense was a phone call. AI voice cloning goes straight at that defense. The scammer supplies the email, the new bank details and the phone number, then answers in a voice that sounds right. Perry and Perry Builders did call. The office manager dialed the number in the fraudulent message and reached the fraudster. No AI was named in that case, and none was needed. Add a cloned voice and even a careful bookkeeper hears confirmation. The weak point is where the phone number came from.

The fix costs nothing and slows you down a little. Every bank change or new payee gets a call to a number you already had: the vendor record in QuickBooks, a signed contract, last year's invoice. A second person approves the first payment to any changed account. Any call from you asking to move money needs a code phrase you agreed in person. Then call your broker and get the social engineering limit in writing, plus any callback condition. The trade-off is a vendor waiting a day for a first payment. That beats learning your limit after the wire clears.

The play

  1. You, today: pull your cyber and crime policies and find the line for social engineering, fraudulent instruction or funds transfer fraud. Write down the limit, the retention and any callback condition.
  2. Your bookkeeper, this week: for your top 20 vendors, record a phone number from a source you trust, a signed contract or an old invoice, in QuickBooks or Xero. That is the only number staff call to confirm a change.
  3. Call your banker and ask for dual approval on new payees and changed accounts in your business banking portal. The person who takes a request never approves it.
  4. Agree a code phrase in person with everyone who can move money. A call from you asking for a payment without it gets a polite no and a callback to your cell.
  5. Paste any payment-change email into ChatGPT or Claude: "This email asks us to change where we send payments. List every sign it is a fraud attempt, which details I should verify and how, and write a short reply saying we will confirm by calling the number we already have on file."
  6. Run The Callback Rule on this page to write the one-page rule, name the second approver and post it next to whoever pays the bills.

03 / PriceBrief · 45 sec

Your $200 ChatGPT Pro buys half as much from October 30.

What moved

At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI reopened its $200 ChatGPT Pro plan with half the usage. On October 30, existing subscribers drop from 20 times the Plus allowance to 10 times in ChatGPT Work and Codex. GPT-6 Pro chat falls from 200 to 100 messages a week. A new Pro 500 plan gives 25 times Plus. On October 15, Google said its $4.99 AI Plus plan loses Pro models on November 13.

What it changes

The $200 plan used to be the bulk deal, at about $10 per Plus-sized unit of usage. Now every Pro tier works out to about $20 a unit, so buying bigger buys you nothing. If your heaviest user ran under a quarter of the old allowance, Pro 100 at 5 times Plus covers them and saves $1,200 a year. If they ran near the old limit, the same work now takes the $500 plan.

What not to do

Do not let the plan renew on autopilot; the invoice shows the same $200, so nothing flags the cut. Do not jump to Pro 500 before you spend the $2,500 credit grant, which runs out December 31. Do not keep Pro 200 for someone who never came near the old limit; Pro 100 covers a quarter of it for half the price. Do not let a $4.99 Google AI Plus seat turn into $19.99 on the company card without a reason.

The tape

Apr 9OpenAI launches a $100 ChatGPT Pro plan with 5 times the Codex usage of Plus. The $200 tier carries 20 times Plus, which makes it the cheaper plan per unit of usage.
Sep 10OpenAI pauses new sign-ups for the $200 Pro plan, citing overwhelming demand for GPT-6 Astra. Existing subscribers keep their 20 times Plus allowance while sign-ups are closed.
Sep 29At DevDay, Pro 200 reopens at 10 times Plus and Pro 500 launches at $500 with 25 times Plus and Ultrafast. Existing Pro 200 subscribers get a one-time $2,500 credit grant that expires December 31.
Oct 15Google emails subscribers that its $4.99 AI Plus plan loses manual choice of Pro models in the Gemini app and AI Mode on November 13. Keeping them takes AI Pro at $19.99, four times the price.
Oct 30Existing Pro 200 subscribers drop to 10 times Plus in ChatGPT Work and Codex, and GPT-6 Pro chat falls to 100 messages a week. Still open: the Google cut on November 13 and the credit expiry on December 31.

The bulk discount quietly left

OpenAI prices its personal plans in multiples of Plus. Through October 29, $200 buys 20 multiples, about $10 each. From October 30 it buys 10, about $20 each, the same rate as Pro 100 at 5 and Pro 500 at 25. OpenAI says the new allowance equals half the old one in API dollar value. The invoice does not change, so your card statement shows nothing. What changes is the week: your heaviest user runs out twice as fast. Google made the same move. AI Plus keeps its $4.99 price, but on November 13 its subscribers drop to Flash models unless they pay $19.99 for AI Pro.

Sort your Pro users this week, before October 30. Ask each one how close they came to the limit in their busiest week of October. Under a quarter of the old allowance, move them to Pro 100 and save $100 a month. Between a quarter and half, keep Pro 200. Above half, keep Pro 200 through December and burn the $2,500 credit grant first, since it only draws once the included allowance runs out. Decide on Pro 500 in January with real numbers. The trade-off is plain. Dropping a tier saves money, but a heavy month stalls the work, so downgrade only the people with headroom.

The play

  1. You, today: pull every ChatGPT Pro charge from the company card and expense reports. Write down who holds each seat and whether it is the $100, $200 or $500 plan.
  2. Each Pro 200 user, before October 30: open Settings, then Usage, in ChatGPT, or Usage and Billing in the Codex app. Confirm the $2,500 credit grant landed and screenshot the balance for your records.
  3. Each Pro user, pasted into ChatGPT: "Here is what I used ChatGPT Pro for in my busiest week of October: [paste the list]. Sort it by how much usage each job took. Mark which jobs a Plus account would handle. Then tell me whether my week fits 5, 10 or 25 times the Plus allowance."
  4. Your office manager: search the card statements for Google AI Plus at $4.99. Ask each holder whether they pick Pro models in Gemini. Upgrade only the ones who do, and let the rest run on Flash after November 13.
  5. You: set a calendar reminder for December 15 to check each credit balance before it expires on December 31, and a second for January 5 to decide on Pro 500 with a full month of real usage.
  6. Run The Shrink Gauge on this page with your Pro seat count and your heaviest user's share of the old limit. It names the plan that keeps the same work going and prices it for the year.

04 / Skip itBrief · 45 sec

Skip the AI layoff. Three in ten who tried it rehired.

What moved

On September 9 Gartner predicted that 30 percent of positions eliminated in AI layoffs will be refilled by 2029, at higher cost. Robert Half already counts 3 in 10 employers who cut positions after adopting AI and later added them back. Careerminds, an outplacement firm, surveyed 600 HR professionals in February, so those numbers are a vendor's. 35.6 percent had rehired more than half the roles cut. Only 26.7 percent came out ahead on cost.

What it changes

The pitch prices an AI agent against a salary. The surveys say the savings rarely survive the real work. In the Careerminds data, 54.6 percent found the new systems needed more human oversight than they expected, and a third lost critical skills. At your size, one cut is often the only person who knows the pricing exceptions and the customer history. When that person leaves, nobody checks the AI except you.

What not to do

Do not cut a role on the strength of a vendor demo; run the AI beside the person for 60 days first. Do not sign an AI agent contract priced against a salary without asking, in writing, who checks its work and for how many hours. Do not let the one person who knows your exceptions leave before those exceptions are written down. Do not count hours saved without counting the hours spent checking.

The tape

Feb 2Gartner publishes its October survey of 321 customer service leaders. Only 20 percent had cut agent staff because of AI. Gartner predicts half of the companies that cut for AI will rehire for similar work by 2027.
Feb 19Careerminds, an outplacement firm, publishes its survey of 600 HR professionals who ran layoffs in the past year. 35.6 percent rehired more than half the roles cut in AI-led layoffs, and 30.9 percent spent more rehiring than they saved.
May 4Robert Half reports from its survey of more than 2,000 hiring managers that 3 in 10 employers who eliminated positions after implementing AI later added those roles back, because the work needed more human judgment than expected.
Sep 9Gartner predicts 30 percent of positions eliminated in AI layoffs will be refilled by 2029, with recruiting, training and onboarding costs on top. Its analyst Tori Paulman puts the reversal rate for enterprises near 40 percent.
NextGartner's customer service rehire forecast lands in 2027. Before you sign an AI agent priced against a salary, run the tool beside the person for 60 days and decide on the Friday log, not the demo.

The checking is the hidden salary

Here is what the replacement math leaves out. An AI tool drafts the invoice, the reply or the schedule in seconds. Someone still reads it, catches the wrong price and handles the odd case. In the Careerminds survey, 30.9 percent spent more rehiring than the cuts saved, and another 42.4 percent only broke even. Gartner found just 20 percent of customer service leaders had cut agent staff for AI at all. It expects half of the companies that did to rehire for similar work by 2027, under new titles. The checking is a job. Somebody ends up doing it, and on a team of twenty that somebody is you.

Keep the person and change the job. Pick the role you were pitched to replace. Give that person ChatGPT, Claude or Copilot for 60 days on the tasks that eat their week. Every Friday they log hours saved and hours spent checking. At day 60 you decide on that log, not on a demo. If the tool frees real hours, move them to work that brings in money: follow-ups, collections, quotes. If you still want a smaller team, let the next resignation do it. The trade-off: two more months of salary. Three in ten employers who cut for AI in Robert Half's survey ended up hiring for the role twice.

The play

  1. You, this week: name the role you were pitched to replace with AI, and list the four or five tasks that eat its week, with hours next to each.
  2. Paste that list into ChatGPT or Claude with: "Here are the tasks my [role] does each week, with hours: [list]. For each task, tell me which part an AI tool can draft, which part a person must check, and what goes wrong for a customer if nobody checks. Give me a table."
  3. The person in the role: runs the AI on those tasks for 60 days and writes three numbers every Friday: hours saved, hours spent checking, mistakes caught.
  4. You, before day one: have that person write down the exceptions only they know, such as pricing deals, problem customers and vendor quirks. Keep the document whatever you decide.
  5. If a vendor pitches an AI agent as a hire, email them: who checks its work, how many hours that takes, who pays when it sends a wrong price, and how you cancel inside 60 days.
  6. Use The Trial Run on this page to write the 60-day plan, the Friday log and the day-60 decision rule in one document.

05 / Steal thisBrief · 45 sec

Your $7 Google Workspace plan now logs leads and drafts replies.

What moved

Google Workspace Studio, the flow builder that ships with every Business plan, picked up the parts an inquiry inbox needs. A Reply to email step reached Gmail users from September 14. On October 13 Google started rolling out a setting that skips follow-up replies, nested if-then branches and a step that searches Drive for a document. Studio costs nothing extra on Business Starter, $7 a user a month. Each person gets 25 flows.

What it changes

Lead intake stops being somebody's afternoon. One flow watches info@, asks Gemini whether a message is a real request for work, pulls out name, phone, address and job type, adds a row to a Sheet, labels the thread, pings your estimator in Chat and leaves a reply in Drafts. Nobody retypes a lead and nothing sits unread over a weekend. You read one Sheet on Monday instead of forty emails.

What not to do

Do not switch on the Send a reply step in week one; one misread email gets a confident answer in your name. Do not build on Ask a Gem; Google closed it to new flows, and those flows stop working no sooner than March 1, 2027. Do not test on the shared inbox; a test run takes real actions. Do not point five flows at one inbox; each person gets 25 active Gmail flows and a daily run cap Google does not publish.

The tape

Sep 2Google adds four steps to Workspace Studio flows: copy a Drive file, move a Drive file, reply in a Gmail thread and reply in Chat. The Gmail step reaches users from September 14 on Business Starter, Standard and Plus.
Sep 30Google closes the Ask a Gem step to new flows as Gems give way to skills. Existing flows keep running and stay editable. They stop working no sooner than March 1, 2027, and have to be rebuilt by hand.
Oct 9Google announces nested conditionals, a Gmail setting that skips follow-up replies and a Search for a Doc step in Drive. Rollout starts October 13 for Rapid and Scheduled Release domains and takes up to 15 days to show.
Oct 17SuperDupr's plan review confirms Studio costs nothing extra on Business Starter, Standard and Plus. Google's limits page caps each person at 25 flows, 25 of them started by Gmail, plus a daily run cap it does not publish.
NextSkills, the Gems replacement, are rolling out in the Gemini app through mid-November, with the Workspace version delayed. Build your flow on Ask Gemini, Decide and Extract now and the March 2027 cutoff never touches it.

Gemini reads the first email only

Here is the mechanism. The starter is When I get an email, filtered to the address your leads land in. Turn on Skip Replies so the flow fires once per conversation, not on every back and forth. A Decide step asks Gemini one yes or no question, whether this is a request for paid work. Extract pulls the fields you name into variables. Everything after that is plain plumbing: Add a row, Add or remove labels, Notify me in Chat, Draft an email. Gemini does not see your other steps, so every prompt has to quote the email body or the extracted fields.

Build it on your own seat first, against old emails you forward to yourself. Run it a week with the reply on draft only, and read every row against the email that produced it. When Decide gets the same kind of message wrong twice, rewrite its question and leave the rest alone. The trade-off is reach against cost. Studio's built-in steps live in Gmail, Drive, Sheets, Chat and Calendar. Zapier and Make reach your CRM and QuickBooks, at a monthly fee. If your leads live in Gmail and a Sheet, Studio covers it free. If they must land in a CRM, keep Zapier for that last hop.

The play

  1. You, today: in admin.google.com confirm Workspace Studio and Gemini are on for your domain. Business Starter, Standard and Plus include Studio at no extra cost. Set steps that share data outside the company to require approval.
  2. Whoever owns the inbox: create three Gmail labels, New lead, Not a lead and Check this, and a Google Sheet called Leads with columns for date, name, phone, address, job type, timeline and subject.
  3. Go to studio.workspace.google.com, choose Start from scratch, pick When I get an email, filter it to your leads address and turn Skip Replies on. Then add Decide, Extract, Add a row, Add or remove labels, Notify me in Chat and Draft an email.
  4. Paste this into the Decide step: "Read this email: [email body]. Answer yes only if a person or business is asking us to quote, schedule or do paid work. Answer no for vendors, job seekers, invoices, newsletters and spam."
  5. Before it touches the shared inbox, forward three old emails to your own seat: a real lead, a vendor pitch and a spam message. Click Test run on each, and check the row, the label and the draft.
  6. Use The Intake Flow on this page to write the full flow spec, the Extract field list and the draft reply for your trade. Keep the reply on draft until two Mondays in a row need no correction.
