01 / Good NewsBrief · 45 sec

Good news, and a catch. There is $166 billion of tariff money going back to the businesses that paid it, and it moves only for the ones who ask.

What moved

The Supreme Court ruled in February that the emergency powers act gives no tariff authority, making roughly $166 billion in duties refundable across 53 million entries and more than 330,000 importers. Customs built a self-service portal and pays with interest. A July court filing shows about $122 billion accepted and 9,837 approved refunds untransmitted because no bank details were on file.

What it changes

Around 126,000 claims have been filed against 330,000 eligible importers. It is not a lawsuit and not a consultant engagement. It is a call to your customs broker and a file upload. Imported $120,000 of goods in 2025 and the duty at 10 to 50 percent plausibly ran $15,000 to $45,000. Refunds land in 60 to 90 days.

What not to do

Do not wait for it to arrive, because customs pushes money to nobody who has not declared. Do not file a post-summary correction, which is the wrong instrument and jams the claim. Do not assume your broker already filed, since brokers do this as a billable service and generally act only when asked.

The tape

Feb 20The Supreme Court rules 6-3 that the emergency powers act authorises no tariffs, making roughly $166 billion collected since early 2025 refundable across 330,000 importers.
Apr 20The self-service portal opens, covering unliquidated entries and those liquidated 80 days or less, roughly 63 percent of all entries. Declarations cannot be amended once filed.
May 29Customs reverses course and says it cannot refund finally liquidated entries without a judgment naming that importer. The government appeals the universal refund order days later.
Jul 14A court filing shows about $122 billion accepted and 9,837 approved refunds sitting untransmitted purely because no bank account information was provided.
NowEntries filed in autumn 2025 are liquidating through August and September. Each one that crosses 80 days moves from a file upload to a lawsuit.

What this teaches you about money you are owed

There is no published claim-by date, and that is the trap rather than the mercy. The clock is liquidation, which happens to each entry on its own about 314 days after filing. The portal covers entries unliquidated or liquidated 80 days or less. Past that, customs reversed its position in May and now says it cannot pay without a judgment naming that importer. Entries filed last autumn are liquidating right now. You do not miss this deadline. You age out quietly, one entry at a time, and then it takes a lawsuit.

The lesson generalises well past tariffs, and it is worth taking as a standing rule. Systems that owe you money almost never pay the people who are owed. They pay the people who ask, in the format asked for, inside a window nobody announces. Nearly ten thousand refunds in this programme are approved, funded and frozen because a business never entered bank details in a portal it had not logged into. That is not bureaucracy failing. That is bureaucracy working exactly as designed, and the design assumes you show up. Anywhere money should be flowing back to you, ask who has to move first, and assume it is you.

The play

  1. Call your customs broker and ask three things: were we the importer of record, what entries and duty do we have, and is our account enrolled for electronic refunds.
  2. Log into the customs portal and confirm bank details are on file. That single omission is why almost ten thousand approved refunds have not moved.
  3. Have the broker submit the declaration. It cannot be amended after filing, so let them check the entry list before it goes.
  4. Never imported, but paid tariff surcharge lines to a distributor? Send the supplier letter the tool writes. No deadline, no downside, and almost nobody is asking.

02 / Bad NewsBrief · 45 sec

Bad news for anyone who pulls permits. The fake invoice carries your real permit number, your real job address and your planner's real name, because all three are public records.

What moved

The FBI issued a nationwide alert on March 9 about criminals impersonating city and county permit officials. The campaign never stopped. Nevada County warned again on July 31, Humboldt County on July 14 for the third time this year, and Rapid City on July 2 after four businesses reported the emails. Contractors, developers and business owners are named as the targets.

What it changes

Documented losses run $3,800 to $13,050, deliberately sized to sit inside a plausible permit fee. One applicant paid $3,800 and got a second demand for $2,800. Payment is requested by wire, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal or crypto, all gone the moment they leave. Government impersonation losses reached $797.9 million last year across 32,424 complaints, roughly double the year before.

What not to do

Do not verify by checking whether the details look right, because the details are always right. Do not call the number on the invoice or reply to the email, which is the one instruction the sender needs you to follow. Do not let urgency skip the rule, since the deadline pressure in the message is the product rather than a coincidence.

The tape

Mar 9The FBI issues alert I-030926-PSA, warning that criminals nationwide are impersonating city and county officials and invoicing permit applicants for wire, peer-to-peer or crypto payment.
Mar 26Baltimore reporting documents a completed loss: an applicant sent $3,800, then received a follow-up demand for $2,800. A Frederick County applicant lost thousands.
Jul 2Rapid City reports at least four businesses received the emails, naming contractors, developers and business owners as the target set.
Jul 14Humboldt County warns for the third time this year. Researchers publish the money trail the same day: 53 mule accounts across 23 campaigns, 55 percent concentrated in two banks.
Jul 31Nevada County publishes the spoofed sender address and states it never asks for payment by wire. Three weeks ago, and still running.

What this teaches you about verification

Your permit application, the hearing agenda and the case file are public records. Anyone can read your permit number, your job address, your project description and the name and title of the planner assigned to you. So when an invoice arrives carrying all of it, the instinct that fires is exactly backwards: nobody else would know that, therefore this is real. Everybody knows it. The county publishes it. Generative tools handle the letterhead. Accuracy is the cheapest part of this fraud to manufacture, which is precisely why they lead with it.

Take the lesson wider than permits, because it applies to every payment request that will ever reach you. Verifying content is worthless when the content is public. Verify the channel instead, because the channel is the part the criminal cannot fake. No United States city or county accepts wire, Zelle, Venmo, PayPal or cryptocurrency for permit fees, so the channel alone settles it with no judgment required. Build your controls on the things an outsider cannot control, never on the things they can simply look up. Detail is not evidence. Route is evidence.

The play

  1. Say one sentence to whoever pulls permits and whoever pays invoices: no permit fee is ever paid from an emailed invoice, only through the portal we navigate to ourselves or in person.
  2. Print the rule card the tool writes and tape it where invoices get paid, so the decision is already made before anyone is under time pressure.
  3. If a permit invoice is in the inbox now, check two things only: does the sender end in .gov or .us, and what payment channel is requested.
  4. If money already went out, call the bank fraud line today and file at ic3.gov. The first 48 hours decide whether any of it comes back.

Detail is not evidence. Route is evidence. Build the control on what an outsider cannot fake.

03 / Good NewsBrief · 45 sec

Good news if you sit near the line. The SBA proposed rewriting every size standard on August 20, and about 114,541 more businesses would count as small.

What moved

On August 20 the SBA proposed replacing the size standards for 338 industry groups, consolidating a table of roughly 995 thresholds. It proposes to reduce no standard, removes all exceptions, and moves 65 industries from a revenue test to a headcount test. The net effect is about 114,541 additional businesses classified as small. Comments close September 21.

What it changes

That word is a gate, not a label. It decides whether you qualify for SBA guaranteed lending and for set-aside contracts inside a pool where the government spent over $883 billion last year against a 23 percent small business goal. If your industry moves, you may become eligible for capital you were quietly locked out of, without anything about your business changing.

What not to do

Do not assume this is only for government contractors, because the same table gates 7(a) and 504 lending. Do not wait for a final rule to talk to a lender about what it would open. Do not skip checking your own code on the theory that somebody would tell you, because nobody is going to.

The tape

Dec 19, 2022The last inflation adjustment to the size standards takes effect. The table has been substantially unchanged since then.
Aug 20The SBA publishes the proposed rule and a companion methodology paper: 338 standards in place of roughly 995, and a net 114,541 more firms counted as small.
Sep 21Comments close on both documents. No extension has been signalled.
Still openNo effective date is set. The prior review ran roughly eight months from proposal to final rule, which is the planning window worth assuming.

What this teaches you about definitions

Twenty minutes of work. Find your six-digit industry code, check it against the proposed table, and note three things: whether your threshold moves, whether the measure changes from revenue to headcount, and whether your industry is folded into a broader group. Sixty-five industries switch from being measured by money to being measured by people, which can flip a company's status in either direction while the company itself stays exactly the same size it was yesterday.

The lesson is worth more than this rule. Your access to capital, to contracts and to relief programmes is decided by definitions written somewhere else, and nobody notifies you when you cross a line. Firms discover they qualified two years after they qualified. Set a habit instead: once a year, check the definitions that gate the money you care about, and know which side of each line you are on. That is a calendar entry, not a project, and it is how you stop finding out late.

The play

  1. Find your six-digit industry code and check it against the proposed table. Note whether the threshold moves and whether the measure switches from revenue to headcount.
  2. If the change would make you newly eligible, put it in front of your lender now and ask what it opens. Do not wait for the final rule to have that conversation.
  3. Use the tool to write the lender email, so the question lands as a specific ask rather than a general enquiry.
  4. Put a yearly reminder in the calendar to re-check the definitions that gate your access to capital and contracts.

04 / Bad NewsBrief · 45 sec

Bad news for the trades. Fourteen product categories are proposed for metals tariffs at 15 to 50 percent, and the list reads like a contractor's purchase order.

What moved

Commerce proposed extending steel, aluminium and copper duties to 14 additional derivative categories. The list includes fire extinguishers, floor safes, welding machine parts, heat exchanger parts, hydraulic engine and motor parts, tanker and agricultural trailers, and filled propane, oxygen and propylene cylinders. Most sit at 25 percent, agricultural trailers at 15 percent through 2027, filled steel containers at 50 percent on the container value.

What it changes

If this is adopted, a landscaping or welding shop buying one $18,000 imported trailer plus a cylinder and extinguisher restock is looking at roughly $2,700 to $4,500 in new duty. The comment window closes Thursday. The precedent for timing is not comforting: the previous round added 428 codes at 50 percent and took effect on short notice.

What not to do

Do not assume this is an importer problem, because the duty lands in the price your distributor quotes you. Do not delay a Q4 trailer or cylinder purchase you were already going to make. Do not skip checking your own restock list against the categories on the theory that none of it applies.

The tape

Aug 18, 2025The previous inclusions round adds 428 product codes at 50 percent, covering fasteners, wire, tubing and downstream assemblies. Precedent for how fast these land.
Aug 6Commerce publishes the notice proposing 14 additional derivative categories under docket BIS-2026-0331, with rates from 15 to 50 percent.
Aug 27The comment window closes on Thursday. Commerce and the trade representative decide after it shuts, and no effective date has been published.

What this teaches you about the gap

The useful thing about a proposed rule is that it is proposed. There is a window between the announcement and the cost, and that window is the only place a small operator gets to act. Right now the gap is days. Pull the purchase you were going to make in the fourth quarter into this one, if the item is on the list and the cash allows it. That is not speculation, it is a dated cost you can see coming with a price attached.

The habit worth building is simple. Cost changes are almost always announced before they land, in places nobody reads, and the announcement is the whole opportunity. Keep one list of the goods your business actually buys in volume, and check it against proposed rules once a month. Most months the answer is nothing. The month it is not, you get several weeks of warning on a number that would otherwise appear in a quote with no explanation attached.

The play

  1. Take your Q4 purchase list and check it against the categories: trailers, filled cylinders, fire extinguishers, floor safes, welding machine parts, heat exchanger parts, hydraulic engine parts.
  2. Price the exposure on the right, then decide whether to pull any of those purchases forward into this month.
  3. Ask your distributor directly whether they expect to pass this through and when. Their answer tells you more about your Q4 pricing than the rule does.
  4. Start the one-page list of what you buy in volume, and set a monthly reminder to check it against proposed rules.

05 / Bad NewsBrief · 45 sec

Bad news about a policy you may already own. The fraud endorsement pays only if you made the verification call, and most owners bought the endorsement and skipped the call.

What moved

An insurer's 2026 claims report puts business email compromise and funds transfer fraud together at 58 percent of cyber incidents last year, with funds transfer fraud alone at 27 percent of claims and an average loss of $141,000. In August a South Carolina town sent $545,000 after someone impersonating a contractor's project manager asked to move from cheque to electronic payment.

What it changes

Standard crime policies frequently do not respond, by design. Employee theft fails because the employee is a victim. Computer fraud fails because a person made the decision. Funds transfer fraud fails because the instruction to the bank was genuine. The endorsement sold to close that gap carries sublimits of $25,000 to $250,000 and conditions payment on you having performed callback verification.

What not to do

Do not assume the bank absorbs it, because a real employee authorised it and there is nothing to reverse. Do not assume your policy responds without reading the endorsement. Do not treat the callback as optional once you have bought the coverage, because it is the condition the insurer will test.

The tape

Mar 5An insurer's claims report puts funds transfer fraud at 27 percent of all cyber claims with an average loss of $141,000, and notes 39 percent involved no confirmed email compromise at all.
Apr 14A payments fraud survey finds 76 percent of United States organisations faced attempted or actual payments fraud last year, with 48 percent of smaller firms taking a real loss.
Jul 7Construction counsel publishes the three clauses that fail on these losses, and the sublimits and verification conditions attached to the endorsement meant to fix it.
Aug 5A South Carolina town sends $545,000 after an impersonator posing as a contractor's project manager requests a switch from cheque to electronic payment.

What this teaches you about protection you bought

The endorsement does not simply pay when you are defrauded. It pays when you were defrauded and you had followed the verification procedure the policy names. One insured lost its claim for exactly that reason, having failed the procedure its own policy required. So the callback is not merely the thing that would have prevented the loss. It is the condition of being paid when prevention fails. Skip it and you have bought a policy engineered not to respond to you.

The wider lesson: protection you purchased often carries obligations you never adopted, and the moment you need it is the moment those obligations get read aloud. Coverage, warranties, guarantees and service agreements all work this way. Once a year, take the protections you pay for and find the sentence that says what you must do to keep them. It is usually one clause and it is usually short. That single reading is worth more than most of what people buy the coverage for.

The play

  1. Read your own endorsement this week and find two things: the sublimit, and the verification procedure it requires. Both are usually on one page.
  2. Price the gap on the right against your largest routine vendor payment. The answer is what you are carrying yourself.
  3. Put the callback rule in writing: any change to where money goes is verified by voice, to the number you already had, before the request arrived.
  4. Save the bank fraud line in the phone of whoever cuts payments, with the words hold and freeze beside it.
Sources
