01 / The DeadlineBrief · 45 sec

Shopify force-upgrades your thank-you page on Wednesday. Any tracking still living in Additional Scripts stops firing, and your ad platforms will not tell you.

What moved

Shopify auto-upgrades legacy Thank You and Order Status pages on August 26. The Additional Scripts box has been read-only since August 28, 2025, so any conversion pixel pasted there is frozen and stops firing at the upgrade. Basic, Shopify, Advanced, Pause and Build plans are all in scope. Plus stores are not. Separately, Managed Markets dropped Delivered Duty Unpaid support on August 24.

What it changes

Your storefront keeps selling and orders keep arriving, so nothing looks broken. The first symptom is a week of missing purchase events in Google Ads and Meta, which means both systems bid against incomplete data going into your fourth quarter. Upgrade by hand before Wednesday and you keep a 30-day revert. Let the automatic upgrade fire first and that revert is gone for good.

What not to do

Do not assume a working checkout means working tracking, because the two fail separately and only one of them is visible. Do not wait for an agency to catch it, since the failure shows up as soft performance rather than an error. Do not switch off Managed Markets purely to dodge the duty change without pricing what you give up, including merchant of record status.

The tape

Aug 28, 2025Shopify makes the Additional Scripts field read-only. Anything already pasted there keeps running but can no longer be edited, which quietly starts the clock on every legacy pixel.
Aug 11, 2026Shopify posts the Managed Markets change, giving 13 days notice that Delivered Duty Unpaid support ends and affected markets convert to Delivered Duty Paid.
Aug 24, 2026Managed Markets stops supporting Delivered Duty Unpaid. International buyers now see duties added at checkout rather than paying on delivery.
Aug 26, 2026Legacy Thank You and Order Status pages are auto-upgraded on Basic, Shopify, Advanced, Pause and Build plans. The 30-day revert is forfeited for anyone who did not upgrade by hand.
Aug 31, 2026Storefront MCP cart tools sunset, closing the same month with a third dated change to anything custom you bolted onto checkout.

The failure that looks like a bad quarter

Most owners will read this and check their storefront, see orders landing, and move on. That is the trap. The checkout and the measurement layer are separate systems, and Wednesday only breaks the second one. What you will see is a fortnight of weaker reported return on ad spend, followed by the algorithms actually delivering weaker results, because you have been feeding them a partial picture of who bought. By the time the numbers look wrong enough to investigate, you have spent real money teaching two ad platforms the wrong lesson about your customers.

The fix is an hour in Settings, not a project. Open Checkout, run the upgrade guide Shopify generates for your store, then open Customer Events and re-register Google Ads, Meta and GA4 as Web Pixels rather than pasted scripts. Place a live test order and confirm the purchase event actually arrives in each platform before you close the tab. The trade-off worth naming: doing it manually costs you an hour now and preserves a 30-day rollback. Doing nothing costs you nothing today and removes the rollback permanently.

The play

  1. The owner or whoever has Shopify admin opens Settings, then Checkout, and runs the personalised upgrade guide today rather than Wednesday, which preserves the 30-day revert.
  2. Open Settings, then Customer Events. Re-register Google Ads, Meta and GA4 as Web Pixels. A pixel that only exists in Additional Scripts does not survive the upgrade.
  3. Place one real test order at the lowest price you sell. Confirm the purchase event lands in Google Ads and Meta, not just in Shopify. Refund it after.
  4. Run the calculator on the right with your monthly ad spend to price what a silent tracking gap costs before anyone in the business would notice it.

02 / The ExposureBrief · 45 sec

Live Stripe secret keys for roughly 660 merchants were published last Tuesday. Stripe was not breached. The keys came out of the merchants' own code.

What moved

On August 18 an actor posted a 35GB archive containing live Stripe secret keys for around 660 merchant accounts along with 688,363 customer records across 42 countries. Of those accounts, 519 could both accept payments and issue payouts. Separate research published the next day found more than 50,000 Stripe keys sitting in public code and on misconfigured servers.

What it changes

A live secret key with payout rights is not a data-protection problem, it is access to your settlement balance and your full customer list. Researchers took one exposed key, pulled the customer list, created payment links and ran a test charge inside 17 hours. United States merchants were the largest group at 212 accounts. The keys leaked from repositories and build logs, not from Stripe.

What not to do

Do not wait to hear from Stripe, because your key leaving your own repository is not something they can see. Do not assume a developer who left three years ago took their hardcoded key with them. Do not replace one unrestricted key with another unrestricted key, which resets the clock without reducing what the next leak costs you.

The tape

Jan 2022The earliest transactions in the leaked archive. The dataset spans four and a half years, which is how long some of these keys have been exposed and working.
Aug 18, 2026A 35GB archive of 17,654 files appears on a data-trading forum, carrying around 650 live secret keys and 688,363 customer records.
Aug 19, 2026Separate research reports more than 50,000 exposed Stripe keys across public repositories, build logs and 3,000 misconfigured servers, roughly 12 percent of them working.
Still openThe actor claims to hold about 20,000 keys with further releases planned. That claim is unverified, but rotation costs you half an hour either way.

Why this reaches shops with no developer

The exposure follows a pattern that has nothing to do with sophistication. Someone builds your store or your booking flow, pastes a secret key into a config file to get it working, and commits it. The repository goes public years later, or a build log gets indexed, or a server is left open. The business changes hands, the contractor moves on, and the key stays live the entire time. Twelve percent of the misconfigured servers researchers scanned held working keys. None of those owners knew, because nothing in their dashboard changes when a key escapes.

The work is half an hour and you do not need the original developer. In the Stripe Dashboard, open Developers, then API keys, and roll the live secret key. Replace it with restricted keys scoped to what each integration actually does, so a checkout key cannot move money out. Then confirm your payout bank account is unchanged and switch on email alerts for account changes. The trade-off is honest: rolling the key will break any integration still using the old one, which is exactly how you find out what is quietly connected to your money.

The play

  1. Open the Stripe Dashboard, go to Developers then API keys, and roll the live secret key today. Do this even if you are sure you are not on the list.
  2. Replace the rolled key with restricted keys scoped per integration. A key that only creates charges cannot also change where your payouts land.
  3. Check Settings for your payout bank account and confirm the details are yours. Turn on notifications for account and bank changes while you are in there.
  4. Send the note the generator on the right writes to whoever built or maintains your site, and require written confirmation that no key sits in code or config.

03 / The CliffBrief · 45 sec

El Salvador work permits stop counting on September 9 and Ukraine on October 19. Your I-9 binder needs new dates written into it this week.

What moved

Temporary Protected Status for El Salvador and every benefit attached to it terminates on September 9, 2026. Ukraine terminates October 19. Immigration services auto-extended a specific list of already-expired work permits up to those dates and no further. Employer guidance landed across August 18 to 21 telling employers to write the new expiration dates into existing I-9 forms now.

What it changes

Around 152,000 Salvadoran permit holders are in the workforce, concentrated in construction, maintenance, transportation, manufacturing and food service. That is this reader's payroll. Paperwork violations run $288 to $2,861 per form. Continuing to employ someone past expiry runs $716 to $5,724 per worker on a first offence. The audit has to be document-based, sorted by permit category, never by nationality.

What not to do

Do not sort your binder by name or by country, which is discrimination and creates a second problem on top of the first. Do not terminate anyone in advance on an assumption, because authorisation runs until it actually expires. Do not leave the reverification date in someone's head instead of a calendar, since September 9 is sixteen days out and payroll will be busy.

The tape

Jun 25, 2026The Supreme Court decides Mullin v. Doe, clearing the path for these terminations to proceed on schedule rather than being held up in litigation.
Aug 12, 2026Immigration services issue the El Salvador alert confirming the designation and related benefits terminate September 9, and listing the eight permit expiration dates covered by the auto-extension.
Aug 14, 2026The Ukraine alert follows, extending permits expiring April 19, 2025 and October 19, 2023 through October 19, 2026 and no further.
Aug 18 to 21, 2026Employer guidance lands from multiple firms telling employers to audit by document rather than by nationality, and to write the new dates in now.
Sep 9, 2026El Salvador terminates. From this date, continuing to employ an affected worker without new documentation is a knowing-employment violation.

A folder, a pen and a calendar entry

This is the rare compliance story with no software in it. You pull the I-9 binder, sort for permit category codes A-12 and C-19, and for anyone holding one of the eight listed El Salvador expiration dates you write September 9, 2026 into the expiration field and note the immigration alert in the additional information box. Ukraine holders with the two listed dates get October 19, 2026. Then each of those names goes into a calendar with a reverification date attached. An hour of work, done once, by whoever keeps the folder.

The harder part is the conversation, and it is worth having early rather than on September 8. A worker whose authorisation lapses is not someone you can keep on payroll, and pretending otherwise converts a staffing problem into a knowing-employment violation with a per-worker price. Tell the affected people now what date applies to them and what documentation would change it, so they have two weeks to act rather than two days. You lose the same worker either way if nothing changes, but you keep the relationship and you keep the record clean.

The play

  1. Whoever keeps the I-9 binder pulls it this week and sorts by permit category code, not by name and not by country of origin.
  2. For each affected form, write the new expiration date into the expiration field and reference the immigration alert in the additional information box.
  3. Put every affected name into a shared calendar with its reverification date, so the deadline does not live only in one person's memory.
  4. Run the calculator on the right to price what one unreverified form costs, then tell affected staff their date this week rather than in September.

04 / The NumberBrief · 45 sec

Diesel hit $5.454 a gallon last week and the surcharge table was rebuilt on August 10. Every quote you write this week without an escalator eats the difference.

What moved

The national on-highway diesel average reached $5.454 a gallon for the week of August 17, up 19.7 cents in a week and $1.741 on the year, after the Strait of Hormuz ceasefire lapsed on August 17. UPS had already rebuilt its Ground surcharge table on August 10, moving the diesel pivot from $4.45 to $4.99 and the surcharge at that pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent.

What it changes

Those two facts compound. Above the new pivot, every 9 cents of diesel moves the surcharge a quarter point. Below it, the same quarter point takes 27 cents. Surcharges climb fast and fall slowly by design. Carriers reset weekly off the prior week's print, so the $5.454 number lands on invoices dated this week, and any fixed quote you issue now carries it for the life of the job.

What not to do

Do not quote a job lasting more than a fortnight without a fuel escalator clause, however awkward it feels to add one. Do not leave a free-shipping threshold set at a number you chose when diesel was under four dollars. Do not assume regional parity, because California was at $6.785 the same week the Gulf Coast sat at $5.237.

The tape

Aug 10, 2026The Ground surcharge table is rebuilt. The diesel pivot moves from $4.45 to $4.99, the surcharge at pivot from 24.50 to 26.00 percent, and the structural floor from 20.25 to 21.25 percent.
Aug 17, 2026The Strait of Hormuz ceasefire lapses. Diesel prints $5.454 a gallon, up 19.7 cents on the week and $1.741 on the year, with United States diesel topping $100 a barrel for the first time.
Aug 20, 2026The national gasoline average reaches $4.1044, three cents up on the week against $3.1337 a year earlier, the highest ever recorded on that date.
Aug 24, 2026Carriers reset off the prior week's print, so the $5.454 figure lands on this week's surcharge. The reset repeats every Monday, with a second carrier resetting Wednesdays.

The ratchet is built to be asymmetric

The part most owners miss is that the surcharge table is not symmetrical, and that is not an accident. Above the $4.99 pivot it takes 9 cents of diesel movement to add a quarter point of surcharge. Below it, the same quarter point requires 27 cents. Fuel spikes therefore reach your invoice in days, and fuel relief reaches it over months. Ground parcel fuel surcharges rose 26.7 percent year on year in the first quarter while diesel itself rose 10 percent. The gap between those two numbers is the mechanism doing its work.

The practical response is to stop treating fuel as a cost you absorb and start treating it as a line you pass through. Add an escalator to any quote valid beyond fourteen days, worded simply: the price holds while the national diesel average stays under a stated number, and adjusts by a stated amount per ten cents above it. Reprice your delivery fee or free-shipping threshold against current diesel rather than against the number you set last year. The trade-off is a slightly harder conversation at quote time in exchange for not funding someone else's freight.

The play

  1. Add a fuel escalator to every quote valid beyond fourteen days this week. State the diesel level the price holds at and the adjustment per ten cents above it.
  2. Pull last month's carrier invoice and find the fuel surcharge line as a percentage of the base rate. If nobody in the business knows that number, that is the finding.
  3. Reprice the delivery fee or free-shipping threshold on your store against diesel at $5.45, not against whatever it was when you set the number.
  4. Use the calculator on the right with your fleet's real mileage to size the year-on-year fuel move before you renew any fixed-price service contract.

05 / The RewriteBrief · 45 sec

The SBA proposed replacing every small-business size standard on August 20. Comments close September 21, and that table decides whether you qualify at all.

What moved

On August 20 the SBA published a proposed rule replacing the size standards for 338 industry groups and industries, collapsing a table of roughly 995 thresholds and moving 65 industries from a revenue test to an employee test. It proposes no reductions, removes all exceptions, and adds a productivity growth adjustment for the first time. Comments close September 21.

What it changes

The rule reclassifies a net 114,541 additional firms as small. If you sit near your threshold, this decides whether you can access SBA guaranteed lending and set-aside contracts inside a pool where the government spent over $883 billion last year against a 23 percent small-business goal. If your industry is folded into a broader group, you may find yourself competing against much larger firms for the same work.

What not to do

Do not assume this is for government contractors only, because the same table gates SBA 7(a) and 504 lending. Do not skip checking your own code on the theory that someone will tell you. Do not treat the comment as a legal filing requiring counsel, since it is a web form on a public docket and the window closes in four weeks.

The tape

Dec 19, 2022The last inflation adjustment to the size standards takes effect, finalised the following July. The current table has been substantially unchanged since then.
Aug 20, 2026The SBA publishes the proposed rule and a companion methodology paper, proposing 338 standards in place of roughly 995 and a net 114,541 more firms classified as small.
Sep 21, 2026Comments close on both documents. The SBA has not signalled an extension, so this is the free lever and it closes in four weeks.
Still openNo effective date has been set. The prior review took roughly eight months from proposal to final rule, which is the planning window worth assuming.

The rare rule that is actually about you

Most federal rulemaking that reaches a small operator is about something else and catches them incidentally. This one is about them by construction. The size standard attached to your industry code is the single line that determines whether the word small applies to your business in the eyes of a lender or a contracting officer, and the SBA is proposing to rewrite all of it at once. Sixty-five industries move from being measured by revenue to being measured by headcount, which can flip a firm's status in either direction without anything about the firm changing.

The work is twenty minutes. Find your six-digit industry code, check it against the proposed tables in the rule, and see whether your threshold moves, whether your measure changes from revenue to employees, and whether your industry is being folded into a broader group. If the change helps you, note the date it would take effect and talk to your lender. If it hurts you, file a comment on the docket saying so, in your own words, with your own numbers. Agencies count comments from actual affected businesses differently from comments filed by trade associations.

The play

  1. Find your six-digit industry code, then check it against the proposed table in the rule. Note whether the threshold moves and whether the measure changes from revenue to employees.
  2. If the change would make you newly eligible, put it in front of your lender now and ask what it would open up, rather than waiting for a final rule.
  3. If the change would push you into a group with much larger competitors, file a comment on the docket. It is a web form, it is free, and it closes September 21.
  4. Use the generator on the right to write that comment with your own numbers in it, then paste it into the docket rather than sending a trade association template.
